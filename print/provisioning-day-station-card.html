Provisioning Day

Tailgate to freezer. The fridge door is the finish line, and on an August quay it is one hour away.

5 °CChilled food at 5 °C or below when received, and held there. Eggs from a van at 7 °C or less.
1 hourAbove 32 °C, nothing sits out of refrigeration for more than one hour; two hours below that.
−18 °CFrozen is −18 °C at every point. Shipped frozen, arrives frozen, or it stays on the quay.

The morning reading. Before coffee, probe the warmest shelf of every cold unit and write the probe's number in the log — not the door display's. Above 5 °C: move the contents, find the cause, write both down. Freezer at −18 °C or lower. Sign the line; the master's documented inspection reads it.

Refuse on paperThis crate reads nine degrees; it goes back, and I am writing it on the note. The reading and the refusal go on the delivery note before the driver leaves — a written 9 °C is rarely argued with, a phone call the next day always is.
Label ruleName · received · opened — three words in marker on every pack before it reaches a shelf. An opened chilled ready-to-eat item is used within seven days at 5 °C; the day of opening is day one. Thaw in the fridge at 5 °C or under running water at 21 °C or below, never on the counter.
