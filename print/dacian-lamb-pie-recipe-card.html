Dacian Lamb Pie — Millet as the Sponge, Baked Under the Bell

Knife-chopped lamb salted an hour ahead, soaked sheep’s cheese and scalded millet in a wholemeal butter crust, baked covered then open — the millet drinks the juice, so the base stays crisp and the slice stands. A reconstruction from Dacian excavations, not a recovered recipe.

Yield: 2 × 28 cm (12 wedges) Active: 1 hr 10 Total: 3 hr 30 Oven: 220→200°C (425→400°F) Done: 80°C centre
Where it was bornDacia, 1st century BC to 106 AD — reconstructed. The larder is excavated: bread wheat at Sarmizegetusa Regia, garlic cloves, millet cooked wet at Șimleu Silvaniei; the Greeks mocked the Thracians as butter-eaters.
Eaten, authenticallyNo Dacian pie survives; the words came with Rome. Plăcintă is Latin placenta, țest is testum: Cato’s sheep’s-cheese pie, baked on a hot hearth under a heated clay bell, coals on top.
How it’s used nowPlăcintă and the țest are still Romanian. Here: two round pies for the guests, the same filling as a lidded slab for the crew.

Crust: wholemeal flour 100% · boiling water 45% · butter 30% · salt 1.5%. Millet is sized to the meat, where the juice comes from; the cheese brings its own salt.

Kit: two heavy 28 cm pans, 4 cm deep (cast iron or deep tins) + two more to upturn as lids, or foil · baking steel or heavy tray · sharp knife · clean cloth · rolling pin · probe thermometer · scale

Method

  1. Soak the cheese. 3 cm pieces, 3× their volume of cold water, fridge 2 h, water changed twice. A crumb should taste milky, lightly salted. Squeeze hard in a cloth; crumble to 1 cm. Salt diffuses back out into fresh water; small pieces and changed water keep it moving. — Cato, De Agri Cultura 76
  2. Chop and salt the lamb. Sinew and thick fat seams out; 1 cm strips, then 5–8 mm pieces. Work in the 20g salt and the garlic by hand 2 min, until tacky. Cover, fridge 1 h (up to 24 h). Salt draws out myosin, which sets on heating into the bind that lets a filling slice. — Offer & Trinick 1983; Ruusunen & Puolanne 2005
  3. Scald the millet. Toast it in the 40g butter, medium heat, 3–4 min, until nutty and the first grains pop. Add the 160g boiling water, stir once, lid on, off the heat 10 min. Spread to cool. It should taste underdone. Proso millet starch gelatinises at 71.5–79°C; lamb collagen shrinks and sheds juice at 53–63°C. Scalded, the grain goes in thirsty and finishes on the juice. — Li et al. 2020; Tornberg 2005
  4. Fill and close. Mix lamb, cold millet and cheese until even. Fry a walnut of it; taste; adjust salt. Butter the pans, line with the bases (overhang on), ~1.2 kg filling a pie, pressed level and firm, no air. Lid on, fold the overhang over, crimp. 1 cm steam hole; brush with butter.
  5. Bake under the bell, then open. Oven 220°C (fan 200) with a steel on the lowest shelf, 20 min. Pies on the steel, an upturned pan or tight foil dome over each: 30 min. Uncover, 200°C (fan 180), 25–35 min, until the lid is deep brown and the centre reads 80°C. Covered it steams and heats through from the hot steel; open, the lid dries and browns. 71°C makes minced lamb safe; 80°C finishes the millet. — Cato 76; USDA FSIS
  6. Rest, then cut. 20 min in the pans on a rack; six wedges a pie (eight for a guest lunch).
The hot-water butter crust — make it after step 3, roll it warm
  1. a.Flour and salt in a bowl. Bring the 340g water and 225g butter to a full boil together; pour over; mix, then knead 1 min while hot.
  2. b.Cover, rest 15 min, still warm. One 660g piece a pie: 380g rolled to a 34 cm base, 280g to a 29 cm lid, ~4 mm.
  3. c.Cold, it cracks: keep it covered, work fast, patch tears with a pinch of dough.
Done: lid deep brown, dry, hollow when tapped · 80°C (176°F) through the steam hole · juice at the vent thick and glossy, not thin and pink.
Serve: warm, after the 20-min rest — cut hot and the slice slumps.
One filling, two dishes — the crew slab Lamb neck or mutton, chopped and salted the same; millet up to 12% of the meat. Deep buttered 2/3 GN (or a ~35 × 30 cm tin), no base, half the crust as a lid tucked inside the rim, steam holes every 10 cm. Same bake to 80°C; rest 20 min; twelve squares. Half the pastry, the same slice.

Elevation

Tier 1 — No Extra Time
  • Bay leaves under the base — Cato set his placenta on them
  • Flaky salt — on the buttered lid before the bake
Tier 2 — Worth It
  • Dried plums — 150g chopped through the filling
  • The Roman finish — 20g honey on the hot lids
Tier 3 — Service
  • A clay țest — heated on the steel, set over the pie
  • Dill + lovage — the modern Romanian table, not Dacian

Troubleshoot

Puddle, soggy baseToo little millet, wet cheese, cut early → 10% millet, squeeze dry, rest 20 min
Gritty milletWent in raw, or centre under 80°C → scald it; bake to 80
Slice crumblesNo salting time, lamb too lean → 1 h with the salt, keep the fat
Too saltyCheese not soaked → 2 h, three waters; fry-test before closing
Dough cracksIt cooled → roll warm, covered; patch with a pinch
Lid pale, base darkCovered too long → uncover at 30 min; up a shelf for the last 10

Charter Prep & Storage

Day beforeSoak + squeeze the cheese; chop + salt the lamb; both covered at ≤4°C
CrustOn the day; or line the pans and chill, covered, up to 24 h
Raw piesFreeze in the pans 1 month; thaw overnight; +10 min under the bell
BakedFridge within 2 h, 3 days; reheat covered at 180°C to 74°C centre
ProvisioningMillet keeps months sealed, brined cheese in its brine; lamb last port
NeverClose the pie untasted, skip the scald, or cut it straight from the oven
