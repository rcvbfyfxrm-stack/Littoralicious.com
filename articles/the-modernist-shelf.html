The Modernist Shelf: What Is Inside Every Book Worth Owning

Every Modernist Cuisine book, volume by volume; Disfrutar, Compartir, Tickets, Candy, Roca and Camarena, book by book; and the free texts that hold the doses. What each one puts in a chef's hands, what it leaves out, where it is sold, and why the free PDF of any of them does not exist.

Buy Modernist Cuisine at Home if one book must live in a locker; read Khymos and Baldwin, free, for the dose and the time; buy Roca for the sous-vide tables and hazard plans; buy Disfrutar, Tickets or Candy last, for the plates; and close any page offering any of them as a free PDF.

Most chefs buy the wrong book first: a restaurant volume that shows the plate and assumes the dose. This guide opens every book worth owning, the Modernist Cuisine library and the Barcelona shelf: what is inside, who it is for, what it lacks. Then the free texts. Free marks a text you can download today under its author's licence; every other link goes to the publisher, the author's shop or a named bookseller. Where to start

Three steps, in this order; most galleys can stop after the first.

The order matters more than the titles: the dose before the plate, the reason before the expensive book.

  1. Read the free shelf. Khymos gives the grams, Baldwin the time and temperature, the Cooking Issues primers the reason each powder behaves as it does. Together they cover every technique below.
  2. Buy one reference, sized to the boat. Modernist Cuisine at Home, 4.5 kg, fits a locker; the full Modernist Cuisine, 23.6 kg, only with a real shelf: it is the one place where sous vide, gels, foams, spheres and nitrogen share a single set of units. Both are opened below, volume by volume.
  3. Buy a plate book last. Disfrutar, Tickets or Candy, once the technique is already in your hands: they show what a world-class team does with it, and they assume you know how. The exception is Roca's sous-vide book, which earns its place early for its tables and its hazard plans.

The Modernist Cuisine library

Six cookbooks and two books of photographs from Nathan Myhrvold's Cooking Lab in Seattle, and what each one puts in your hands.

The Lab sells all of them from its own shop in dollars; outside North America Phaidon distributes them. None has a legal ebook: the one app, Modernist Cuisine at Home for the iPad, is no longer sold or updated.

  • Modernist Cuisine: The Art and Science of Cooking — Myhrvold, Chris Young and Maxime Bilet, 2011. Five volumes and a kitchen manual in a steel case, 2,438 pages, more than 1,500 recipes, 23.6 kg. $625 from the Lab, $525 at Kitchen Arts & Letters, £395 from Phaidon. What you get, volume by volume:
    • Volume 1, History and Fundamentals: microbiology for cooks, food safety, food and health, heat and energy, the physics of food and water. The chapters a galley needs most and reads least.
    • Volume 2, Techniques and Equipment: traditional cooking, modern ovens, the sous-vide chapter (packing, equipment, chilling and reheating, searing after the bath) and The Modernist Kitchen: extracting, juicing, filtering, concentrating, drying, and the cryogenic freezing that is the book's nitrogen.
    • Volume 3, Animals and Plants: meat and seafood, salted, marinated, smoked or restructured; plant foods, cooked sous vide, under pressure, fried or re-textured.
    • Volume 4, Ingredients and Preparations: thickeners, gels with spherification inside them, emulsions, foams, then wine and coffee. The dose volume.
    • Volume 5, Plated-Dish Recipes: tender and tough cuts, poultry, fish, shellfish, eggs, starches, fruit and vegetables.
    • Kitchen Manual: every recipe again, spiral-bound, waterproof and tear-proof. The only volume that goes near the stove.
    Its honest limit, in the publisher's own FAQ: some recipes need liquid nitrogen, a centrifuge or a rotor-stator homogeniser, and the authors chose not to simplify them.
  • Modernist Cuisine at Home — Myhrvold and Bilet, 2012. One volume of 456 pages and a 228-page wire-bound, water-resistant manual; more than 400 recipes, 114 step-by-step tutorials, 4.5 kg; $140. Part one is the kit: countertop tools, conventional gear, sous vide, ingredients. Part two starts with the basics, stocks, sauces, brines and spice mixes, then runs through dishes: steak, carnitas, braised short ribs, roast chicken, pizza, mac and cheese, risotto and paella, dishes for the microwave, custards and pies. What a chef takes from it: pressure-cooker stocks, sous vide in a pot with a probe, everything in grams. The publisher calls it the less complex book and sends you to the full set for the deeper science, food safety and advanced techniques.
  • Modernist Bread — Myhrvold and Francisco Migoya, 2017. Five volumes and a 430-page manual in a steel case, more than 2,600 pages, 22.7 kg, built on more than 1,600 experiments; $625. History and fundamentals; ingredients; techniques and equipment, from fermentation to cooling and storage; lean, enriched, rye and whole-grain breads; flatbreads, pizza, bagels, pretzels, bao and gluten-free. James Beard Award, 2018. For the boat that bakes every morning, and has the shelf.
  • Modernist Bread at Home — Myhrvold and Migoya, 2024. 420 pages including a 172-page wire-bound manual, more than 160 recipes; $140. Gear, scheduling, starters and getting the most from a domestic oven, then breads from lean French loaves to bao and gluten-free. A yacht oven is a domestic oven, which makes this the bread book for a galley: it shows how to rescue over-proofed dough and how to plan a sourdough around a day that belongs to the guests.
  • Modernist Pizza — Myhrvold and Migoya, 2021. Three volumes and a 412-page manual, 1,708 pages, 1,016 recipes, 16.1 kg; $425. History and the science of dough and ovens; dough, sauce, cheese and toppings; the recipes. The pages a chef uses are the oven ones: how heat moves in a home oven and how to make it bake like a hotter one.
  • The Photography of Modernist Cuisine (2013, 312 pages, $120) and Food & Drink (2023, more than 200 photographs, $99): no recipes. How the cutaway pictures were made, for the chef who shoots the plate. The Lab's eighth book, Natural Wonders (2025), is landscapes, not food.

The Barcelona shelf

The books on the list, and what is inside each one; buy them for the plates, once the technique is yours.

The Cooking Lab explains the technique; these show what the best teams in the city do with it. Read them for the thinking, and expect them to assume you already cook at that level.

  • Disfrutar, Volumes 1 to 3 — Oriol Castro, Eduard Xatruch and Mateu Casañas, Abalon Books. The three ran elBulli's kitchen, creative work and pastry before they opened their own restaurant. Each volume is two books in a slipcase: a small one on how they think, and a ring-bound catalogue of the dishes, metric, written for professionals.
    • Volume 1, 2014–2017: 166 recipes and more than 200 photographs; gatefolds open out wherever a technique is unusual, so you see every step. A foreword by Ferran Adrià. English edition sold out at Kitchen Arts & Letters; the edition on sale at Casa del Libro.
    • Volume 2, 2018–2020: 96 dishes, among them a puff pastry made without flour and croquants made in the microwave. The binder's pages come out for the kitchen, and about fifty QR codes lead to videos of the hands at work. English edition sold out in New York.
    • Volume 3, 2021–2023: the years of the third Michelin star, ending a year before No. 1 on the World's 50 Best. 98 recipes, many with video, and the Living Table, where the table itself becomes part of the service. $350 at Kitchen Arts & Letters, in stock.
  • Compartir — the same three, Planeta Gastro, 2017, 216 pages, more than 200 photographs by Francesc Guillamet, prologue by Ferran Adrià. The story of the move from elBulli to Cadaqués, and the fifty dishes of a restaurant built for sharing: every plate is designed for the whole table, which is how a yacht lunch is served. The cheapest way into how they think. Spanish and Catalan only. Planeta.
  • Tapas, The Tickets Cuisine — Albert Adrià, RBA Libros, 2013, 176 pages. The emblematic tapas of Tickets, which Adrià called "a neighbourhood Bulli", written for cooking at home. Modern technique at tapa scale, which is canapé scale. €35 signed, in English, from albertadria.com.
  • Tickets Evolution — Albert Adrià, RBA Libros, 2018, 272 pages, nearly 100 recipes. In the author's words, "this is not a tapas book": the dishes that outgrew the format, among them the strawberry tree with elderflower and kimchi, prawns in frozen salt, saffron sponge with bread soup, and a grilled oyster with black chanterelle tea. €35 signed, in English, from albertadria.com; a Spanish EPUB at Casa del Libro.
  • Candy. Los postres de elBarri — David Gil and Albert Adrià, RBA Libros, 2021, 512 pages, 25.5 by 32 cm. Ten years of desserts, 2011 to 2021, from the group of restaurants that included Tickets, Pakta, Hoja Santa and Enigma. It comes in two halves: a book of photographs, each dessert with a short note on its idea, and a recipe book with every step. The sweet side of foams and texturisers. Spanish only. €85 signed from albertadria.com; the cheapest legal digital book on this page, EPUB, €12.99 at Casa del Libro.
  • Natura — Albert Adrià, elBulli Books, 2008, 151 pages, out of stock at the author's own shop; and elBulli 2005–2011 — Ferran Adrià, Phaidon, 2014, seven volumes, 2,720 pages, from the elBullifoundation store: the archive these techniques came from, for the history rather than the galley.

Sous vide, and the line that keeps it safe

One free guide does more than the two famous books, and the safety rules are in none of them.

The temperatures are easy to find; the holding rules are not, and at sea the holding is the whole risk.

  • Free: A Practical Guide to Sous Vide Cooking by Douglas Baldwin, a mathematician, at douglasbaldwin.com: the time-at-temperature tables, pasteurisation included, that the paid books quote. His 2012 peer-reviewed review is free on the same site. If you read one thing on sous vide, read this.
  • Sous-Vide Cuisine / La cocina al vacío — Joan Roca and Salvador Brugués, Montagud Editores, Barcelona. Spanish 2003, out of print; English 2005, 192 pages; bilingual 2014, 256 pages. The restaurant that taught Spain to cook in a bag, writing down its own method: the equipment and materials, the principles, then precise times and temperatures by ingredient, and plated dishes built on them. The 2014 bilingual edition widens the tables to many more ingredients, adds a brining stage for many of them, and keeps the sample HACCP plans: the one cookbook on this page with a hazard plan you can adapt for a yacht's food-safety file. €110 from the Celler de Can Roca shop, $188 bilingual at Kitchen Arts & Letters. No ebook.
  • In the paid shelf, Modernist Cuisine Volume 2, chapter 9, is the fullest treatment: packing, chilling, reheating, searing.
  • Under Pressure: Cooking Sous Vide — Thomas Keller, Artisan, 2008, 295 pages. The American restaurant version, a book of finished dishes. Hachette.
  • Free: the Low-Temperature and Sous-Vide Primer, Cooking Issues, for the reasoning in plain words.
  • Free: the safety texts no cookbook prints. FDA Hazards Guide, chapter 13: fish in a pouch held below 3.3 °C from packing to plate. FSA 2016 guidance on vacuum-packed chilled foods: ten days unless another barrier is proven. What that means for a boat fridge is in the sister piece.

Broth

The one technique the modernist shelf does not treat as a technique, and the one book that does.

Every book on this page assumes a stock; this one is about nothing else.

  • Caldos = Broths. El código del sabor — Ricard Camarena, Montagud Editores, November 2015, 272 pages, bilingual Spanish and English, two kilos. His juices were the spine of his Valencia menu before the book existed, used as seasonings rather than bases. The book opens on four rules, here in short: a broth thought of as a preliminary comes out discreet, so make it as a finished dish; make it as if it were the only thing you will give your mother for lunch; the broth is the result, so it must be worth dipping bread in; and never use a poor one because the other ingredients will cover it. Then the methods behind them. Casa del Libro; sold out at Kitchen Arts & Letters, $95 when in stock.
  • On Food and Cooking, below, for what happens in the pot; no free text on broth is worth the name.

The dose: gels, foams, spheres, textures

Where to find the grams per litre, and how far to trust them.

For a working chef this is the shelf that earns its keep, and most of it costs nothing. In the paid shelf the same ground is Modernist Cuisine Volume 4.

  • Free: Texture: A hydrocolloid recipe collection, edited by Martin Lersch, version 3.0, 2014, 138 pages, Creative Commons, from khymos.org. Its quiet strength: within each chapter the recipes run in ascending order of hydrocolloid, every dose also given as a mass percentage, so you can see at a glance where a gel turns from wobble to slice. Its honest limit, stated by the editor: the recipes were gathered, not tested by him. Use it as the starting dose, then test before service.
  • Free: the Hydrocolloids Primer, Cooking Issues: ten powders, what each one does, the usual range, direct, reverse and agar cold-oil spheres. Read it beside Khymos: one gives the why, the other the number.
  • Free: Sosa's Texture Wheel, The mousse revolution, Naturfoam and Gracila Gel booklets at sosa.cat. The dose for their own products, which is what most galleys actually stock; a maker's sheet, so it never tells you a rival powder would do.
  • Ideas in Food — Aki Kamozawa and H. Alexander Talbot, Clarkson Potter, 2010, 320 pages. The working cook's book: technique as a way of thinking, not a catalogue. For the chef who has the doses and wants to invent with them. Penguin Random House.
  • Handbook of Hydrocolloids, third edition — Phillips and Williams, Woodhead/Elsevier, 2020, a chapter per hydrocolloid. Written for food scientists, priced for libraries; the book to borrow the day a gel fails and nothing above explains why. Elsevier.

The why

The books that explain what the powder and the bath are actually doing.

The dose tells you what works; these tell you what to change when it does not.

  • On Food and Cooking — Harold McGee, Scribner, revised edition 2004, 896 pages. Start here if you read one book of science: heat, proteins, starch, emulsions, in prose a tired chef can follow. Every author on this page cites it. Record and sellers.
  • Molecular Gastronomy: Exploring the Science of Flavour — Hervé This, Columbia University Press, 2006. The field's founder explaining his own questions, one short chapter each; for the curious more than the practical. Columbia.
  • Handbook of Molecular Gastronomy — Burke, Kelly, Lavelle and This, CRC Press, 2021, 894 pages. The paper behind the plate, by the people who did the work: Baldwin on sous vide, Barham on cryogenics, three chapters on spherification, one of them the laboratory measurement of calcium moving into an alginate gel. Academic in tone and price; for the chef who teaches, or wants to. Routledge.
  • Free: the open textbooks, for the vocabulary: Chemistry of Cooking on LibreTexts; Modern Pastry and Plated Dessert Techniques and Understanding Ingredients for the Canadian Baker from BCcampus, both CC BY, both with a PDF. Written for culinary students, which is exactly their use: the words, defined once and correctly.
  • Free: Harvard's Science and Cooking lecture series: the physics spoken by the chefs themselves, for a long passage with a screen.

Nitrogen

There is no nitrogen cookbook worth the money; two chapters and two free texts cover it.

Read these before the dewar comes aboard, not after.

  • Modernist Cuisine, Volume 2, chapter 10, cryogenic freezing and carbonating: the paid shelf's nitrogen.
  • Handbook of Molecular Gastronomy, above: Peter Barham's chapter Cryogenics in the Kitchen, and a restaurant chapter on liquid-nitrogen ice creams.
  • Free: the Liquid Nitrogen Primer, Cooking Issues: cold burns, asphyxiation, pressure, dewars, and the line "Do not use LN in an unventilated area".
  • Free: EIGA Doc 44/18, Hazards of Oxygen-Deficient Atmospheres: 19.5 per cent oxygen is the line, and one litre of liquid makes about 0.65 cubic metres of gas.
  • The idea is older than any of these books: in 1901 Agnes Marshall, the London ice-cream maker, proposed freezing ice cream at the table with "liquid air".

One warning before you search for any of these titles.

Watch for: a search for any Disfrutar volume, or for the Roca book, returns a dozen pages offering it as a free PDF or EPUB. None is the book: they are pirated scans or bait, the royalties are the chefs', and a yacht's network is the last place to open an unknown file. The legal digital editions that exist are the two RBA ebooks above; Disfrutar, every Cooking Lab book, Sous-Vide Cuisine and Caldos have none.
