The Modernist Shelf: Four Books Worth Their Weight, and the Six That Cost Nothing

Modernist Cuisine ships at 23.6 kilos and the hydrocolloid collection every chef quotes weighs nothing. Which books actually print the grams per litre, the time at temperature and the oxygen line for sous vide, spheres, foams, texturizers and nitrogen, which editions exist in English, where each one is sold, and why the free PDF of a Disfrutar book does not exist.

Read Baldwin and the Khymos collection tonight for nothing, buy Modernist Cuisine the day the boat has a shelf that takes twenty-four kilos, and close any page offering a Disfrutar book as a free PDF: there is no such file, only a trap.

Six techniques, and for each one the books that go deepest, the free texts that print the same doses and temperatures, and where every title is sold. Free marks a text you can download today under its author's licence; every other link goes to the publisher, the author's shop or a named bookseller, and where a legal ebook exists it is named. Records were checked on 30 and 31 October 2026; prices are those pages' list prices on that date.

The whole shelf

Four books that cover every technique below. Buy one of the first two; the other two are the ground they stand on.

  • Modernist Cuisine: The Art and Science of Cooking — Myhrvold, Young and Bilet, The Cooking Lab, 2011. Five volumes and a kitchen manual, 2,438 pages, 23.6 kg boxed. The only index where sous vide, gels, foams, spheres and nitrogen share one set of units. £395 from Phaidon; no digital edition.
  • Handbook of Molecular Gastronomy — Burke, Kelly, Lavelle and This, CRC Press, 2021, 894 pages. The mechanism behind each technique, written by the people who worked it out: Baldwin on sous vide, Barham on cryogenics, three chapters on spherification. Library price, from Routledge.
  • On Food and Cooking — Harold McGee, Scribner, revised edition 2004, 896 pages. The book every author above cites. Record and sellers.
  • Molecular Gastronomy: Exploring the Science of Flavour — Hervé This, Columbia University Press, 2006. The other one. Columbia.

Sous vide

The book a restaurant wrote in 2003, and the free guide the paid books cite.

  • Sous-Vide Cuisine / La cocina al vacío — Joan Roca and Salvador Brugués, Montagud Editores, Barcelona. Spanish first edition 2003, now out of print; English 2005, 192 pages; bilingual edition 2014, 256 pages. €110 in Spanish, English or French from the Celler de Can Roca shop, $188 bilingual at Kitchen Arts & Letters. No ebook.
  • Under Pressure: Cooking Sous Vide — Thomas Keller, Artisan, 2008, 295 pages. The American restaurant version. Hachette.
  • Free: A Practical Guide to Sous Vide Cooking by Douglas Baldwin, a mathematician, with the time-at-temperature tables, at douglasbaldwin.com; his 2012 peer-reviewed review is free on the same site as an author preprint.
  • Free: the Low-Temperature and Sous-Vide Primer by Dave Arnold, Cooking Issues.
  • Free: the safety texts no monograph prints. FDA Fish and Fishery Products Hazards Guide, chapter 13 (fish in a pouch below 3.3 °C from packing to plate) and the FSA 2016 guidance on vacuum-packed chilled foods (ten days unless another barrier is proven).

Foams and the siphon

Modernist Cuisine for the physics, Barcelona for the plates, the makers' sheets for the dose.

  • Candy. Los postres de elBarri — David Gil and Albert Adrià, RBA Libros, 2021, 512 pages. The sweet side of foams and texturizers, ten years of the elBarri desserts; Spanish only. €85 signed from albertadria.com; legal ebook: EPUB, RBA, €12.99 at Casa del Libro.
  • Tapas, The Tickets Cuisine and Tickets Evolution — Albert Adrià, RBA Libros, 2013 and 2018, 176 and 272 pages. The Tickets kitchen's recipes, both in English, €35 each signed from albertadria.com; Tickets Evolution also as a Spanish EPUB from Casa del Libro.
  • Free: Texture: A hydrocolloid recipe collection, edited by Martin Lersch, version 3.0, 2014, 138 pages, Creative Commons. The doses in grams per litre, recipe by recipe, foams included. Download from khymos.org.
  • Free: Sosa's one-page Texture Wheel for Gastronomy, The mousse revolution and Naturfoam booklets, the dose by product, at sosa.cat.

Spheres

Disfrutar is where spherification went after elBulli, and the volumes show the hands on video.

  • Disfrutar, Volumes 1 to 3 — Oriol Castro, Eduard Xatruch and Mateu Casañas, Abalon Books. Vol. 1 (2014–2017), 2021, 548 pages with a binder of more than 100 recipes; Vol. 2 (2018–2020), 2023, 96 recipes and 50 QR codes to the videos; Vol. 3 (2021–2023), November 2025. English editions of 2 and 3 at $350 each from Kitchen Arts & Letters; Vol. 1 at Casa del Libro. No ebook of any volume exists.
  • Compartir — the same three, Planeta Gastro, 2017, 216 pages: the fifty dishes of their Cadaqués restaurant before Barcelona; Spanish and Catalan only. Planeta.
  • Handbook of Molecular Gastronomy, above: three chapters on spherification, one of them the laboratory measurement of how calcium moves into an alginate gel.
  • Free: the Hydrocolloids Primer, Cooking Issues: alginate at 0.5 to 1 per cent, the calcium bath at 0.8 to 1.5 per cent, direct, reverse and agar cold-oil spherification. The Khymos collection, above, has the recipes. The hold times for spheres at sea are in the sister piece.

Textures

A chapter per hydrocolloid at library price, or the same doses for nothing.

  • Handbook of Hydrocolloids, third edition — Phillips and Williams, Woodhead/Elsevier, 2020. Agar, carrageenans, alginates, xanthan, gellan, pectins, gelatin, each its own chapter. Elsevier.
  • Ideas in Food — Aki Kamozawa and H. Alexander Talbot, Clarkson Potter, 2010, 320 pages. The working cook's hydrocolloid book. Penguin Random House.
  • Free: Khymos, above, is the dose table. Why the dose matters more than the recipe: agar sets between 32 and 45 °C and holds to 85 to 95 °C, gelatine melts at body heat, and a book that prints the set and melt point with the percentage is one you can scale from six covers to sixty.
  • Free: the open textbooks for the vocabulary: Chemistry of Cooking on LibreTexts, with chapters on non-traditional thickeners and on low-temperature cooking; Modern Pastry and Plated Dessert Techniques and Understanding Ingredients for the Canadian Baker from BCcampus, both CC BY, both with a PDF.
  • Free: Sosa's Gracila Gel recipe book and the Texture Wheel, at sosa.cat; Harvard's Science and Cooking lecture series for the physics spoken by the chefs themselves.

Nitrogen

One chapter by the physicist who wrote the rules, and the free primer that says them plainly.

  • Handbook of Molecular Gastronomy, above: Peter Barham's chapter Cryogenics in the Kitchen, and a restaurant chapter on liquid-nitrogen ice creams. No cookbook in print goes deeper on the physics.
  • Free: the Liquid Nitrogen Primer, Cooking Issues: cold burns, asphyxiation, pressure, dewars, and the line "Do not use LN in an unventilated area".
  • Free: EIGA Doc 44/18, Hazards of Oxygen-Deficient Atmospheres: 19.5 per cent oxygen is the line, and one litre of liquid makes about 0.65 cubic metres of gas. The galley arithmetic is in the sister piece.
  • The idea is older than any of these books: in 1901 Agnes Marshall, the London ice-cream maker, proposed freezing ice cream at the table with "liquid air".

Broth

The one technique the modernist shelf does not treat as a technique, and the one book that does.

  • Caldos = Broths. El código del sabor — Ricard Camarena, Montagud Editores, November 2015, 272 pages, bilingual Spanish and English, two kilos. His juices were the spine of his Valencia menu before the book existed. Casa del Libro; sold out at Kitchen Arts & Letters, $95 when in stock.
  • On Food and Cooking, above, for what happens in the pot; no free text on broth is worth the name.

The rest of the Barcelona shelf

Out of print, or still being written.

  • Natura — Albert Adrià, elBulli Books, 2008, 151 pages. Out of stock at the author's own shop.
  • elBulli 2005–2011 — Ferran Adrià, Phaidon, 2014, seven volumes, 2,720 pages, from the elBullifoundation store.
  • Bullipedia — the elBullifoundation's encyclopaedia, more than thirty 500-page volumes since 2013, still growing, sold by the foundation.

Watch for: a search for any Disfrutar volume, or for the Roca book, returns a dozen pages offering it as a free PDF or EPUB. None is the book: they are pirated scans or bait, the royalties are the chefs', and a yacht's network is the last place to open an unknown file. The legal digital editions that exist are the two RBA ebooks above; Disfrutar, Modernist Cuisine, Sous-Vide Cuisine and Caldos have none.

Tested at sea is owed: the shelf has been read, not yet cooked from on a passage, and the first galley that does will say so in the Weekly Brief.
