Venice’s Ten-Day Window: Six Weeks to the Autumn Moeche, Thirty-Four to the Castraure Overlap

Lagoon moeche averaged €90–100/kg in spring 2025, the worst run the moecanti remember, on a 12-tonne fishery. The autumn moult opens in six weeks; the castraure overlap is the last week of April 2027.

The Alert

The season piece, not the week piece: nothing in the lagoon is soft on 1 September, and that is exactly why you act now.

What’s happening. Lagoon moeche averaged €90–100 a kilo across spring 2025 and printed €130 at Chioggia on 3 April, the worst spring the moecanti could remember (Il Nord Est, 4 April 2025). The fishery lands 11.9 t a year against 200 t a century ago, and the average price has climbed from the €45–50/kg of the 2011–2020 mean to €76 in 2022 (Lazzarini et al., Il Pesce 2/2023, from the Regione Veneto Carta Ittica). About fifty men still fish them, down from 115 in 1998. Evidence Grade: trade-journal compilation of official landings and market data · the whole lagoon · Strength: firm on the tonnage, single-source on the price.

The context. A moeca is a state, not a species. The male green crab, Carcinus aestuarii, sheds twice a year — roughly mid-March to early May, and mid-October to early December — and the serajanti sort the catch by hand: matti go back, boni are three weeks off, spiantani a day or two. Then they wait on the tide, because shore crabs moult at high water on an inbuilt twelve-and-a-half-hour rhythm they keep even in a lightless tank (Abellò, Warman & Naylor 1997). At emergence the blood floods with bursicon, the cuticle-hardening hormone, in one surge (Webster et al. 2013); in the blue crab, the only one timed under a microscope, calcium starts loading at three hours and the mineral fronts meet at five to eight (Dillaman et al. 2005). The moecanti’s rule is twelve hours. After that it is a crab again.

A moeca is twelve hours in the life of a three-centimetre crab, and the lagoon sells about twelve tonnes of those hours a year.

How it hits us. Three different things arrive at the pass as “moeche”: the lagoon crab out of a vieri, at €90–130/kg last spring; the blue crab’s soft-shell, sold as moeche di granchio blu from €70/kg since June 2023; and the name itself, which nobody owns — Slow Food lists the moeca in its Ark of Taste but not as a Presidium, and the IGP file was opened only in 2024. The Zane banco calls the true ones almost impossible to find (Corriere del Veneto, 23 June 2023). The economics explain the scarcity: only 10–15% of the crabs lifted ever become a moeca, so they are 3–4% of the lagoon’s landed weight and 20–25% of its fishing revenue. Buy at the banco, with the moecante’s name attached, or you are buying the word.

Nothing to order this week — the crabs are hard and the castraure eight months off. The position instead. By 15 September, name your moecante: through your Rialto banco (Ittica Zane, Tuesday to Saturday from 07:30, shut Sunday and Monday) or the Cooperativa San Marco at Burano, and ask when the first spiantani go into the cages. When they do, buy alive, by the kilo, for the day: 150 g a head, 1.5 kg for ten; damp cloth, 4 °C, fried the same day, never frozen. For April 2027, phone I Sapori di Sant’Erasmo by 1 April and book castraure — the word, not carciofi — for the last week of April, two or three a head, raw. The trap is the word on the stall: at €70 it is almost certainly blue crab, which is a fine thing and a different one.

Falsifies if: the first castraure reach the Erbaria in the last week of April 2027 with no lagoon moeche on the Rialto stalls — the spring run having closed before the artichoke opened. That is the live case against, and it is a warming one: central Venice is warming at 0.65 °C a decade since 1980 and up to 1.75 °C a decade since 2008, with the lagoon summering 1.2 °C warmer than the sea (Amos et al. 2017; one lagoon · Strength: moderate). A warm March pulls the whole moult forward and leaves an artichoke and an empty cage. Against it: the moecanti’s own tempo says warmth compresses the run rather than removing it — a day in spring against three in the autumn cold — and the spring sorting yields 60–70% boni against 45–50% in late autumn. That favours thin over none.

We report back: in the Weekly Brief after the autumn cages fill (the week of 19 October 2026) — held, missed or overtaken — and again at the horizon, the first week of May 2027, or the week the moecanti call the spring run finished.
