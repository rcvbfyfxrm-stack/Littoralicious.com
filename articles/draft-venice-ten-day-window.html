Venice’s Ten-Day Window: Six Weeks to the Autumn Moeche, Thirty-Four to the Castraure Overlap

Lagoon moeche averaged €90–100/kg in spring 2025, the worst run the moecanti remember, on a 12-tonne fishery. The autumn moult opens in six weeks; the castraure overlap is the last week of April 2027.

Lagoon moeche averaged €90–100 a kilo across spring 2025 and printed €130 at Chioggia on 3 April, the worst spring the moecanti could remember (Il Nord Est, 4 April 2025), on a fishery landing 11.9 t a year against 200 t a century ago (Il Pesce 2/2023). The autumn moult opens in about six weeks. The overlap with the first castraure of Sant’Erasmo is thirty-four weeks out: the last week of April 2027.

This is the season piece, not the week piece: nothing in the lagoon is soft on 1 September. What is decided now is whether you hold a moecante’s number when the cages fill in mid-October, and a grower’s for the ten days next spring when crab and artichoke share a stall.

The Move

The position, not the purchase: three names before 15 September, one booking for April 2027.

Nothing to order this week: the crabs are hard, the castraure eight months off. By 15 September, name your moecante — through your Rialto banco (Ittica Zane, Tuesday to Saturday from 07:30, shut Sunday and Monday — the routine is in Port Call Venice) or the Cooperativa San Marco at Burano — and ask when the first spiantani go into the cages. Buy alive, by the kilo, for the day: 150 g a head, 1.5 kg for ten; damp cloth, 4 °C (39 °F), fried the same day, never frozen — twelve hours on it is a hard crab again. For April 2027, phone I Sapori di Sant’Erasmo (+39 347 059 4687) by 1 April and book castraure — the word, not carciofi — for the last week of April, two or three a head, raw.

Why now: the calendar under it moves for nobody.

Why It’s Happening

Read the moult and the money and you can see next spring’s stall before the moecante does.

The lagoon landed 11.9 t of moeche in 2022 — 12.4 t in 2021, 8.6 t in the pandemic year — against 200 t a year a century ago, and the average price rose from €45–50/kg, the 2011–2020 mean, to €76/kg in 2022 (Lazzarini et al., Il Pesce 2/2023, from the Regione Veneto Carta Ittica 2022 and the market records). Evidence Grade: trade-journal compilation of official landings and market data · coverage = the whole lagoon · Strength: firm on the tonnage, single-source on the price.

Then 2025: one moecante told Il Nord Est that this spring was the worst of all, the average at €90–100/kg, Chioggia at €130 on 3 April. About fifty men still fish moeche, from 115 in 1998 (Il Pesce 2/2023). In autumn 2022 the chefs’ verdict in Il Gazzettino was blunter: the crabs died in the heat.

A moeca is a state, not a species. The male green crab, Carcinus aestuarii, sheds twice a year — roughly mid-March to early May and mid-October to early December; the females moult in early summer and are left alone. The serajanti sort the catch by hand: matti go back; boni, three weeks off, go into the vieri; spiantani, a day or two off, are kept apart. Then they wait on the tide: shore crabs moult at high water on an inbuilt twelve-and-a-half-hour rhythm they keep even in a constant, lightless tank (Abellò, Warman and Naylor 1997, C. maenas) — so the cages are lifted at the turn, not by the clock. A spiantano that turns in a day in spring takes three in the autumn cold (Il Pesce 5/2021).

That is the calendar. The product is the hour after it.

At emergence the crab’s blood floods with bursicon, the cuticle-hardening hormone, in one surge (Webster et al. 2013, C. maenas). In the blue crab, the one crab whose clock has been timed under the microscope, calcium starts loading into the new shell at three hours and the mineral fronts meet at five to eight, a honeycomb already rigid — Callinectes, not Carcinus, so take it as the shape of the curve (Dillaman et al. 2005). The moecanti’s rule is twelve hours; after that it is a crab again. The first laboratory description of the moeca came only in July 2026: 240 soft-shells from the north and south lagoon, 32.6 ± 5.6 mm across the shell, softer, more cohesive and more elastic than hard-shell controls (Bordignon et al. 2026). The softest, the strapasae, never reach a restaurant.

Now the money.

Three things arrive as “moeche”. The lagoon crab out of a vieri, €90–130/kg at the banco last spring. The blue crab’s soft-shell, sold as moeche di granchio blu from €70/kg since June 2023 (La Nuova Venezia) — the Zane banco calls the true ones almost impossible to find (Corriere del Veneto, 23 June 2023). And the name, which nobody owns: Slow Food lists the moeca in its Ark of Taste, not as a Presidium; the IGP file was opened only in 2024. A serajante sells a spiantano at €2–3 a piece; only 10–15% of the crabs lifted become a moeca, so moeche are 3–4% of the lagoon’s landed weight and 20–25% of its fishing revenue, €520,000–670,000 a year (Il Pesce 2/2023; 5/2021). Buy at the banco, with the moecante’s name attached.

Strip the label: the moult, not the menu, decides what you serve.

A moeca is twelve hours in the life of a three-centimetre crab, and the lagoon sells about twelve tonnes of those hours a year.

The Forecast

Where the two calendars meet next spring — the call, how sure, and the one stall that kills it.

Forecast
Overlap holds, thincastraure on the Erbaria and lagoon moeche at Rialto in the same fortnight, 25 April to about 5 May 2027; moeche at €90–130/kg on a run under 15 t
May 2027horizon: the end of the spring overlap
Likelyconfidence — the artichoke’s date is firm to the week; the crab’s volume is watching

Falsifies if: the first castraure reach the Erbaria in the last week of April 2027 with no lagoon moeche on the Rialto stalls — the spring run having closed before the artichoke opened — or the moecanti call the spring run finished before 25 April.

Four readings behind that, each named so you can check it — and mind the basis, the gap between the banco’s price and what the provisioner quotes the boat.

  1. The artichoke’s clock is the plant’s: the Presidium cuts the castraure at the end of April, then runs on the side buds, the botoli, to the second half of June (Slow Food Presidium, Carciofo violetto di Sant’Erasmo); the 2026 season was reported open on 29 April (VeneziaToday). → points to: the artichoke end of the overlap is fixed to the week.
  2. The crab’s spring calendar: males moult mid-March to early May (Il Pesce 5/2021), and the spring sorting yields 60–70% boni against 45–50% in late autumn (Il Pesce 2/2023). → points to: the spring run is the richer one and, in a normal year, still going in the last week of April.
  3. Volume and price: 11.9 t in 2022; €90–100/kg average and €130 at the peak in spring 2025 (Il Nord Est; single-season press report · Strength: provisional). → points to: the overlap exists, thin and dear — booked, not found.
  4. The water: central Venice warming at 0.65 °C a decade since 1980 and up to 1.75 °C a decade since 2008, the lagoon summering 1.2 °C warmer than the sea (Amos et al. 2017; one lagoon · Strength: moderate). → points to: the spring moult creeps earlier — a run over before the artichoke starts.

The other side, at full strength: both failures are heat — autumn 2022, when chefs said the crabs died in the water, and spring 2025, the worst in memory. The blue crab, thinner in Venice than in the Po delta (Ca’ Foscari, 28 July 2023), feeds hardest at 25–28 °C and carries 1.63 million eggs (Casalini et al. 2026, laboratory trials); a lagoon a degree warmer than the sea is its dining room. Then the 2027 run starts in early March on one-day moults and ends by mid-April: an artichoke and an empty cage.

The counter-case in one line: a warm March pulls the whole spring moult forward and the run is finished before the first castraura is cut — an overlap of zero, in a year when the artichoke is on time.

What decides between the two is not opinion but the mechanism under both calendars.

Both clocks run on the same warming water and move early together in a warm spring; the question is whether the run shifts more than the plant. The moecanti’s tempo says warmth compresses the run, not removes it — a day in spring, three in autumn (Il Pesce 5/2021 · Strength: provisional) — and the 60–70% spring sorting rate fills the cages fullest in April. That favours thin over none. Load-bearing figure: Amos 2017, observational, 1980–2015, one lagoon · Strength: moderate.

The call stands: 25 April to about 5 May 2027, both on the stalls, €90–130/kg, booked by name. Firm the morning the first castraure arrive while the Zane banco still has lagoon moeche; watching if the moecanti say in early April that the cages are emptying. Lead time: the moecante knows by early April, the grower by the third week, your provisioner a fortnight later.

At the Provisioning End

Read the table back in one sentence: the crab is dear, brief, and alive or nothing; the artichoke’s date is the only fixed thing on the table — so the position is three names now and a verbal special later.

For the people you feed: moeche fritte — press each live crab lightly to drain it, leave them about two hours in beaten egg, which they drink, flour lightly, fry at 180 °C (356 °F) for four to five minutes, salt, lemon, nothing else; the castraure raw, sliced thin into lemon water, olive oil, a shaving of Parmigiano; the boiled masanete on the crew table the same week. On the guest card it is a verbal special, never a printed line — the tide decides it, and if the cages are empty the cuttlefish in its ink takes the plate without apology.

Make that swap badly and the saving turns into the wrong crab.

The trap is the word. “Moeche” on a stall or an invoice may be a blue-crab soft-shell at €70, or a lagoon crab lifted too late and already stiffening; ask the species and pick one up — it should bend, not click. On the artichoke side the same trick runs every March: false castraure sold before the island has cut one (FreshPlaza, 19 March 2013; Notizie Plus, 17 April 2020), and botoli sold as castraure from May. Never print moeche on a menu.

No supplier holds a tide, so carry the fallback before the window opens.

The hedge is the lagoon’s own second string: cuttlefish with its ink, or schie, on the plate the moeche would have taken; for the artichoke, the botoli through to June, a different bud from the same island and named as such on the card. If a guest wants soft-shell and the cages are empty, the blue-crab moeca is a fair dish named as what it is. The honest uncertainty: we could reach no run report for autumn 2025 or spring 2026; the last season on record is spring 2025, and it was the worst.

You need no terminal; three readings tell you which way it broke:

One: the moecante’s answer on 15 September and again on 1 April — are there spiantani in the cages; a yes in early April keeps the call at likely. Two: the lagoon-moeche price at the Rialto banco — under €90/kg at the end of April flips the volume to firm; over €130 holds it as a thin verbal special; no lagoon moeche in the week the castraure arrive is the falsifier. Three: the Erbaria’s first castraure — the growers cut at the end of April; buds before 20 April mean the overlap has moved early, and you ring the moecante that morning.

The one thing

Name a moecante, a banco and a grower now; buy moeche live, by the kilo, for the day, with the lagoon’s name on the invoice; and plan the crab-and-artichoke plate for the last week of April 2027 as a verbal special — the one reading that changes it is castraure on the Erbaria with no lagoon moeche across the campo.

We report back: in the Weekly Brief after the autumn window closes (the week of 7 December 2026) — held, missed or overtaken, with the Rialto price printed — and again at the horizon (the week of 10 May 2027), or the week the falsifier appears.

Tested at sea.
