The Brief

Palma is an island capital that feeds itself through two trade-only doors — and a weekend rule that empties Monday's fish counters.

Port type: island capital with its own wholesale market and first-sale auction, both closed to non-trade buyers. Fresh food reaches the boat by van, from three kinds of door: Mercapalma, the city's wholesale market at Coll d'en Rabassa, which moved 206,000 tonnes of fresh food in 2022 (about 690 tonnes a day) and sends 80 per cent of it to hotels, restaurants and caterers; the Llotja on the Contramoll Mollet, where OPMallorcamar — the producers' organisation that says it represents 99 per cent of Mallorca's fresh-fish boats — auctions around 700 boxes an hour from 04:30; and the retail markets and shops that buy from both. A yacht buys at the third door, or at the first two through a registered buyer.

In Palma the source is nine minutes away and locked. You buy through the card, not the kilometre.

Two rules shape the fresh-fish week. OPMallorcamar's buyers must be registered with the Balearic port authority and the Directorate-General of Fisheries, and hold a food-supply contract and a deposit before they bid — which is why the fishmonger at Santa Catalina is your route to the auction, not a substitute for it. And the Govern's own release on the llampuga season notes in passing that professional fishing is prohibited at weekends: the boats go out on Monday, so there is little to auction on Monday morning, and Santa Catalina's own timetable says the fish stalls are closed on Mondays. Tuesday's counter is the first honest one of the week.

One day. That is the cumulative time the FDA allows a fishery product at 6–10 °C before Listeria monocytogenes becomes the problem, against seven days at −0.4 to 5 °C; the same table gives Vibrio parahaemolyticus two hours at 22–27 °C and one hour above 27 °C (Fish and Fishery Products Hazards and Controls Guidance, June 2022, Table A-2). A box that leaves the Llotja at 05:00 in August and waits on a Passeig Marítim quay is spending the second row of that table before it reaches your ice.

Cook This Port

From 25 August the island cooks one fish, and it is not on any importer's list.

The catch / crop

Llampuga — the dolphinfish, Coryphaena hippurus — at its peak window from the end of August into November. The Govern opens the llampuguera season on 25 August and closes it on 31 December, with a daily limit of 150 kg per boat and a catch ceiling of 127 tonnes for the fleet; the 2023 season landed 99,486 kg for €592,376, an average first-sale price of about €6 a kilo. It is caught around shade rafts of palm and mastic branches, the capcers, that gather the fish for the encircling net. Local word: llampuga; the dish is llampuga amb pebres.

Serve it

The island way: floured, fried, and served with fried red peppers — llampuga amb pebres, the dish the autumn kitchens are built around. For a yacht service, sear the loin rare and put the peppers underneath as a warm relish with the frying oil, vinegar and a little of the pepper juice. The price reality: press reports from the first days of the 2025 season put the stall price around €12 a kilo; there is no import price to compare it with, because nobody flies llampuga in. Out of season it is simply not on the menu, which is the point of it.

Touch and Go: Hours Alongside

With hours alongside you never leave the boat: the agent is the port, and the provisioner is the van.

Hours alongside in Palma buys you more than in most ports, because two agents keep 24-hour lines in the city and a provisioner publishes next-day delivery to the yacht. What decides it is the paperwork, not the shopping: nobody here publishes a cut-off hour, so the written list with the ETA is the only clock you control.

Send in writing, with your ETA, as early as the itinerary allows: (1) the full provision list with brand and grade specs; (2) the fresh box — species, cut, kilos, and the substitute you accept, with “llampuga if landed” written as a wish, not an order, from 25 August; (3) dietary lines and wine preferences; (4) customs clearance on the fresh box, cold chain on the quay, delivery window in local time — ask the agent to confirm the fresh box is inside their customs coordination, in writing; (5) the boat-show and Copa del Rey exceptions, with the longer lead.

Buy fish on the day, from Tuesday. Never put llampuga, red prawn or any other landing on a printed menu before it is on your ice; name the substitute before you print — a farmed bream or bass from the same counter is the honest one. Order the evening before with the stall that knows the boat, and collect at seven.

But the box only comes off the back shelf if the stall reads you as a chef who cooks here, not a tourist passing through.

Five words and one habit. Bon dia before anything else; per favor when you ask; moltes de gràcies — with the island's de in the middle, which is how the Govern's own courtesy list writes it, and which no visitor says; per al vaixell, for the boat, when they ask how much. Then the habit the market itself names: a puesto de confianza. One stall, every time, the order the evening before, and the question that opens the back stock — què teniu de fresc avui?, what have you got fresh today — asked before you name a species.

Transport — A Van Port

Moving boxes in Palma is a van problem, and the van's three destinations are all under a quarter of an hour away.

The van, not the bus: Mercapalma is 7 km from the Moll Vell and about nine minutes by road, 8–9 km and about twelve minutes from Marina Port de Mallorca or Club de Mar (our estimates, routed on OpenStreetMap); Makro at Son Fuster is on the same side of the city. Crates do not ride the bus: EMT Palma's own rules admit luggage only if it is small and the driver agrees. For a crew member without a box, the single ticket is €2 in the city and the port zone and €5 on the airport lines, by EMT's FAQ of June 2026 — a fare ordinance was in public consultation in August and September 2026, so check the figure at the stop. Line 1 (Portopí–Sindicat) runs the whole Passeig Marítim past Club de Mar and the marinas; line 18 links Portixol with the Fan–Mercapalma stop and the centre.

Taxis and the port supplement: the Ajuntament's posted tariff (Decreto 20/2025, still the table shown in August 2026) is a €2.50 flag-fall and €1.20 a kilometre on weekdays, €2.85 and €1.35 at night and on holidays, plus a €4.65 supplement once per journey to or from the port or the airport — and luggage is carried free, by the same page. luggage free, port +€4.65

Access to the source, and the fee that does not exist: Mercapalma charges a car €1.15 at the gate and sells blocks of entry passes to retailers, but the gate is the least of it — the halls sell to professionals. no account, no purchase Palma levies no day-tripper fee on crew stepping ashore — none appears in the tax authority's list or the city's. The Balearic tourist tax, as the law reads, falls on hotel stays, tourist dwellings and cruise passengers in transit (€2 a day, halved from the ninth day and cut by three quarters from November to April); recreational and charter yachts are not on its list of taxable establishments, and crew are not named in it. The city's loading bays allow goods vehicles thirty minutes unless a sign says otherwise; the port authority's accreditation card is for the fenced commercial zones, not the promenade — each marina's gate rule is the marina's, so ask it.

The days that close everything: Mercapalma's own 2026 list is the one to pin: 1, 6 and 20 January; 2 March; 3 and 6 April; 1 May; 24 June; 15 August; 12 October; 8, 25 and 26 December — and the Balearic calendar adds Maundy Thursday, 2 April. Santa Catalina posts its own closures, 15 August and 1 May among them. Sunday trading is the law's, not the shop's: the Govern allows at most ten trading Sundays and holidays a year (in 2026: 4 and 18 January, 8 March, 2 April, 15 August, 6 September, 29 November, 6, 20 and 26 December), and only small independents, bakeries and shops inside the tourist zones — the old town all year, the Passeig Marítim strip from 15 March to 30 October — open on the others.

The trap is the Sunday-Monday pair. Nothing fresh is landed at the weekend, Mercapalma is shut on Sunday, the markets are shut on Sunday, the big supermarkets outside the tourist zones may open only on the ten authorised days, and Santa Catalina's fish stalls stay shut on Monday. A boat that arrives Saturday afternoon and sails Monday morning provisions from the provisioner's van, or from Sa Llotja Supermercat in the old town — open every day 08:30–23:00, six minutes from the Moll Vell — or not at all.

⚠ Watch-outs by Season & Event

The few dates that break a charter day here — and what each one means to the town.

20 January, Sant Sebastià — the city's patron saint; Mercapalma lists it as a closure. Last days of April — the Palma International Boat Show and its Superyacht Village take over the Moll Vell (28 April to 1 May in 2027, by the organiser's own dates): the galley berth becomes the exhibition ground. 1–8 August — the Copa del Rey MAPFRE fills the Real Club Náutico and the bay with the racing fleet (the 44th edition ran 1–8 August 2026, by the organiser's and the federation's calendars). Every cruise day — the port and the lines have agreed a cap of three cruise ships a day, only one of them over 5,000 berths, and 8,500 berths a day on a weekly average (7,500 from June to September from 2027); Palma took 1,840,006 cruise passengers in 2024, by the port authority's own plan — on a three-ship morning the old town's shops and the taxi rank belong to them until lunch. 25 August — the llampuga season opens, and from then until November the island's counters and menus turn to one fish; Cala Rajada holds its Mostra de la Llampuga on the second weekend of October. 15 August — Mercapalma and Santa Catalina both closed. 19 September — Nit de l'Art, the galleries' night in the old town, thirtieth edition in 2026: the streets around the Moll Vell fill from the evening. 18 October — the Palma Marathon starts at 08:00 from the Parc de la Mar, under the Cathedral, on the Moll Vell's doorstep.

The Bottom Line

Three moves, the load-out, and the days to avoid.

Palma is the yacht port with a wholesale market and a fish auction of its own, both trade-only, and a walkable old town that buys from them. By how long you stay: hours — Estela Shipping with the written list and the ETA, the fresh box blind from YS Delivers; a couple of days — Makro through a hospitality account, plus the quick radius from the Moll Vell: Santo Cristo, Colmado Santo Domingo, Santa Catalina from Tuesday; a week — Mercapalma and the Llotja through the people who hold the cards, with your provisioner as the carrier.

Before you sail away, load llampuga in season (25 August to 31 December, peak into November), a whole Sobrassada de Mallorca with the numbered PGI seal, the ensaïmades ordered the day before and stowed flat, and a case from La Vinoteca collected before 13:30.

Avoid: the boat-show days at the Moll Vell at the end of April, the Copa del Rey week of 1–8 August, three-ship cruise mornings, the marathon morning of 18 October, Mondays for fish, and the thirteen dates on Mercapalma's 2026 list.

The one thing: in Palma the source is nine minutes away and closed to you — settle whose card you are buying on before the boat is alongside, and the rest of the port is a walk.
