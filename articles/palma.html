Palma: Trade-Only at Dawn, Everything Else Within Twenty Minutes

Palma has its own wholesale market and fish auction and both are shut to a yacht by rule; the provisioning guide by how long you stay — the agent to call with hours, the merchants inside fifteen minutes of the Moll Vell with days, and the people who hold the cards with a week.

An island capital with its own wholesale market and its own fish auction, and you are barred from both. Everything else is a walk.

Trade-only before dawn — Mercapalma opens to professionals at 05:40 on Tuesdays and Fridays, and the Llotja runs about 700 boxes an hour from 04:30 to buyers holding a food contract and a deposit. The source is nine minutes from the Moll Vell on foot, and you reach it through somebody else's card.

Settle whose card you are buying on before the boat is alongside. That is the whole port in a sentence: Palma is the rare Mediterranean yacht stop where the fresh food arrives through two doors that will not open for a yacht, and a third that opens for anyone. You buy at the third, or at the first two through a registered buyer — which means the fishmonger at Santa Catalina is your route to the auction, not a substitute for it.

From the Berth

In Palma the port comes to you: two agents on the Passeig Marítim who clear the box and put it on the quay.

Send in writing, with your ETA, as early as the itinerary allows. One: the full provision list with brand and grade specs. Two: the fresh box — species, cut, kilos, and the substitute you accept, with “llampuga if landed” written as a wish and not an order, from 25 August. Three: dietary lines and wine preferences. Four: customs clearance on the fresh box, cold chain on the quay, delivery window in local time — and ask the agent to confirm in writing that the fresh box sits inside their customs coordination. Five: the boat-show and Copa del Rey exceptions, with the longer lead.

The failure here is the phone order. Both agents publish customs coordination; neither publishes a cut-off hour. A fresh box “confirmed” by voice at 18:00 has no delivery window, no substitute list and no customs line attached to it. Write it, send it with the ETA, keep the reply.

No Time — Hours Alongside

You never leave the boat: the provisioner's box, and the four doors inside a ten-minute walk of the Moll Vell.

A Few Hours — Half a Day Ashore

The markets, and the cash-and-carry if you hold a Spanish business card.

Professional fishing is prohibited at weekends, so the boats go out on Monday and there is little to auction on Monday morning — which is why Santa Catalina's own timetable closes its fish stalls that day. Tuesday's counter is the first honest one of the week. And buy on the day, from the stall that knows the boat: never put llampuga, red prawn or any other landing on a printed menu before it is on your ice. Name the substitute before you print — a farmed bream or bass from the same counter is the honest one.

Five words and one habit. Bon dia before anything else; per favor when you ask; moltes de gràcies — with the island's de in the middle, which is how the Govern's own courtesy list writes it and which no visitor says; per al vaixell, for the boat, when they ask how much. Then the habit the market itself names: a puesto de confianza. One stall, every time, the order placed the evening before, and the question that opens the back stock — què teniu de fresc avui? — asked before you name a species.

A Day or More — To the Source

Where the shops buy. Both doors are shut to you, and knowing exactly how they work is what makes your provisioner useful.

Why the transfer matters more than the distance: the FDA allows a fishery product one cumulative day at 6–10 °C before Listeria monocytogenes becomes the problem, against seven days at −0.4 to 5 °C; the same table gives Vibrio parahaemolyticus two hours at 22–27 °C and one hour above 27 (Fish and Fishery Products Hazards and Controls Guidance, June 2022, Table A-2). A box that leaves the Llotja at 05:00 in August and waits on a Passeig Marítim quay is spending the second row of that table before it reaches your ice. Ask the agent where the box sits between the auction and your passerelle, and get the answer in writing.

Cook This Port

From 25 August the island cooks one fish, and nobody flies it in.

Llampuga — the dolphinfish, Coryphaena hippurus — peaks from the end of August into November. The Govern opens the llampuguera season on 25 August and closes it on 31 December, with a daily limit of 150 kg a boat and a fleet ceiling of 127 tonnes; the 2023 season landed 99,486 kg for €592,376, an average first-sale price of about €6 a kilo, and press reports from the opening days of 2025 put the stall price near €12. It is caught around capcers — shade rafts of palm and mastic branches that gather the fish for the encircling net.

The island way is floured, fried and served with fried red peppers: llampuga amb pebres, the dish the autumn kitchens are built around. For a yacht service, sear the loin rare and put the peppers underneath as a warm relish with the frying oil, a little vinegar and some of the pepper juice. There is no import price to compare it against, because nobody flies llampuga in — and out of season it is simply not on the menu, which is the point of it.

Watch-outs

The weeks when the port stops being a port.

The boat show, last days of April: the Moll Vell becomes the exhibition ground. If you are berthed there, your quay is the show. The Copa del Rey, early August: the Passeig Marítim fills and the agents' lead times stretch — ask for both exceptions in the brief. Cruise mornings: the taxi rank at Santa Catalina and the old-town streets fill early. Sundays and holidays: Mercapalma is closed, both markets are closed, and Sa Llotja Supermercat is the one door still open. Mondays: no fish.

Coordinates were geocoded from those addresses against OpenStreetMap on 8 September 2026; several resolved on the venue name itself. Prices move; the season dates do not.
