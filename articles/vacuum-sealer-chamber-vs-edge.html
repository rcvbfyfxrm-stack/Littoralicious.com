Chamber or Edge: The Vacuum Sealer Verdict for a Galley

Two Anova machines stand in for the two kinds of vacuum sealer; the physics that decides liquids, the FDA line that decides raw fish, and a counted study that says what a vacuum actually buys — with the verdict a galley can act on.

The bag is not the preservative, the cold is. Vacuum what is already at 4 °C, and never bag raw fish held warmer than 3.3.

Buy if

Buy the chamber machine ($374, Anova Precision Chamber) if you seal stock, marinades and portions every week and have 34 × 29 cm of bench to give it. Buy the edge sealer ($155, Anova Precision Pro) if what you seal is dry or going straight to the freezer — it is a third of the price and honest about being a different tool. The extra $219 buys one thing: liquids that stay in the bag.

The number that decides the purchase is the one only the chamber publishes. Anova gives the chamber a final pressure and a 70 L/min pump; for the edge machine it gives an extraction rate and no pressure at all — the brochure telling you, politely, that these are not the same tool. Neither sheet gives a weight. And 280 W is about 1.2 A at 230 V for the seconds a cycle runs, so the constraint is never the generator: it is the 22.5 cm of height the chamber needs with its lid up.

Why It Works

Whether liquids stay in the bag is not a setting. It is where the pressure difference sits.

Anova's chamber page says the machine seals “even liquids” and keeps them “in the bag, not on your counter”; its edge page promises an end to sucking liquid into the port by way of a moist setting. The first claim is physics. The second is a mitigation.

The mechanism: an edge sealer pumps air out through the bag's open mouth, so pressure inside falls below the room and the room pushes the contents — liquid first — toward the port. In a chamber the bag lies open inside the box and the whole box is evacuated, so bag and surroundings fall together and nothing is pushed anywhere; the bag is sealed first and the air let back in only then, and it is the returning air that squeezes it tight.

The real ceiling is the liquid's own vapour pressure. Water at 20 °C boils below 23 mbar absolute, at 37 °C below 63 mbar (NIST Chemistry WebBook, Antoine parameters). Anova's −0.95 bar is about 63 mbar absolute at sea level: a room-temperature marinade sits still, a warm stock boils. Henkelman's professional 99.8% air removal is about 2 mbar, where anything wetter than a cold cheese boils — which is exactly why those machines sell a Liquid Control option that stops the pump when the bag begins to foam. Chill what you are about to seal, and the ceiling stops mattering.

What the Test Actually Shows

One published counted trial — and the honest limit of what it proves.

We have not run a counted trial of these two machines and this review will not pretend to. The counted test exists elsewhere: a Polish team packed carp fish balls with a Multivac C200 chamber machine and, for comparison, in air, holding both at +4 °C for fourteen days — 28 sets, two packaging methods across seven sampling days, in duplicate (Kaliniak-Dziura et al., Molecules, 2026).

Twelve days against nine: three days of shelf life bought by taking the air out.

Sensory life at 4 °C ran to 12 days under vacuum against 9 in air, and fat-oxidation markers — peroxide value and TBARS — were lower in the vacuum-packed samples at days 7, 9, 12 and 14. Both sets stayed inside the microbiological limit the authors used, a total viable count under 6 log CFU/g, for the whole fourteen days. So the vacuum bought quality, not safety.

What it does not cover is the galley case. Those were cooked, formed fish balls, not raw fillets, sealed in a chamber machine; the study says nothing about an edge sealer, and nothing about raw fish held at 5 °C for a week.

Watch for: the temperature, not the bag. Clostridium botulinum type E grows and makes toxin from 3.3 °C, and where refrigeration is the only barrier the FDA guidance keeps vacuum-packed refrigerated raw fish below that from packing to consumption (Fish and Fishery Products Hazards and Controls, June 2022, Ch. 13 and Table A-1). A galley fridge at 5 °C is inside the growth range, and a vacuum bag is precisely the anaerobic package the organism wants. The sealer makes a good fridge better and a warm fridge worse.

Alternatives & When Not To

The trade is bench against pressure — and sometimes neither machine wins.

CheaperAnova Precision Vacuum Sealer Pro$155 · $114 on sale, Aug 2026

What you give up: liquids, and any published final pressure. 110 W, 12 L/min, and a moist setting instead of a number.

Best for: rice, flour, coffee, dry-cured meats, and anything going straight to the freezer.

Its own specification sheet is the honest one here: no pressure figure appears because the machine cannot hold the bag and the room at the same pressure, and the maker does not claim it can.

SmallerThe same edge sealer, stowed40 × 18 cm · fits a drawer

What you give up: the wet work — stock, marinade, anything with a sauce on it.

Best for: a galley where stowage matters more than stock. The chamber is a box with a lid and will never fit a drawer.

The bar shape is the whole argument: it lives on its side against a bulkhead, which the chamber cannot do.

Step upHenkelman Boxer 42Dealer-priced · 64 kg

What you give up: the locker, and then some — 53 × 49 × 44 cm, 0.8–1.0 kW, and it weighs more than the chef.

Best for: a crew galley that seals all day and has a bench built for it. A 21 m³/h pump, a 410 mm seal bar, 99.8% air removal.

At 2 mbar it is past the point where most wet food boils, which is why Liquid Control exists as an option rather than a feature.

Skip itIce in a drawer and filmWhat you already have

What you give up: three days on a cooked product, and freezer burn protection.

Best for: a galley whose fridge holds 5 °C, whose freezer is full, and whose fish is eaten within two days of landing.

The FDA's line does not care what bag the fish is in. If you cannot hold 3.3 °C, buying a sealer solves nothing and hides the problem.

Tested at sea — the rule, not these two machines.
