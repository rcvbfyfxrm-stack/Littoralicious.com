elBulli: The Winter Closure That Made the Kitchen, and the 1,846 Dishes It Left Behind

A June 1961 minigolf licence, two French bulldogs, and a kitchen that shut five months a year to think. The record behind the legend, the alginate chemistry, and what a galley can keep.

On the night of 30 July 2011, at the end of the track that climbs out of Roses and drops into Cala Montjoi, elBulli served its last dinner. The final sitting was kept for the staff and their families; the menu was kept secret; the ordinary dinners that season had run to about forty small dishes (BBC News, 30 July 2011). Juli Soler ran the room, as he had since 1981. Ferran Adrià, who had walked into this kitchen as a one-month stagiaire in 1983, was reported by the BBC that day in one sentence: “El Bulli is not closing, it is transforming itself, because its soul is going to remain.” The cameras were there too: the foundation's own chronology files the night under the title of the film that recorded it, El último vals, the last waltz.

elBulli in five lines: a beach bar at Cala Montjoi that began with a minigolf licence dated June 1961 and took its name from the owners' French bulldogs · a cove with no passing trade and no winter custom, so from 1987 the kitchen shut five months a year and used the empty months to invent · genuine to one rule, creativity means not copying, enforced by a numbered catalogue that reached 1,846 dishes · a chemistry of calcium ions locking seaweed chains into a skin, described in 1973 · on board, a logbook, a crew meal cooked with intent, and one protected hour a week for the dish you are still getting wrong.

Origin

A cove nobody passes, a licence for a minigolf, and a French kitchen with two stars before its famous chef ran one.

Why here: Cala Montjoi sits on the south flank of Cap de Creus, reached from Roses by a track. Nobody stumbles in. A restaurant here has one way to fill a room: be worth the drive.

The record begins with a piece of paper: “a planning permission licence for a minigolf installation, dated June 1961,” taken out by Hans and Marketta Schilling (elBulli's own history). A beach bar followed in 1963, a grill-room in 1964, and it took the name the Schillings used for their French bulldogs. Jean-Louis Neichel arrived in 1975 with nouvelle cuisine; the first Michelin star came in 1976. Juli Soler became director in 1981, and two years later, under Jean-Paul Vinay, the guide gave the cove two stars (elBullifoundation chronology).

Every kitchen this famous carries a story cooks repeat. Here is what the record actually says:

The legend: a beach shack where a self-taught genius invented “molecular gastronomy” in a laboratory. The record: the cove held two Michelin stars before Ferran Adrià had ever run a kitchen — one in 1976 under Neichel, two in 1983 under Vinay. Adrià came for a month's work experience in 1983, joined in March 1984, shared the stove with Christian Lutaud from that November, and ran it alone only from 1987, two years after the guide had taken one of those stars back (elBullifoundation chronology). The laboratory came later and by hand: a creative team in 1994, a workshop in 1997. The label was refused by those it was pinned on — Adrià, Blumenthal, Keller and McGee, The Observer, 10 December 2006: “the term ‘molecular gastronomy’ does not describe our cooking, or indeed any style of cooking.”

The People

A couple's bar, a French kitchen, a Catalan maître, two brothers from L'Hospitalet — and the three cooks who hold the No.1 today.

The Schillings kept the bar and the dogs; for the kitchen they hired French. Neichel (1975–1980) brought the first star, Vinay the second. Soler, director from 1981, took on the boy from L'Hospitalet de Llobregat, made him head chef, bought the property and the building with him in 1990, and co-promoted the foundation with him in 2013. Albert Adrià joined his brother in 1985, took the pastry in 1987, and from 1998 directed the workshop in Barcelona while still working the restaurant (elBullifoundation chronology). Oriol Castro, Eduard Xatruch and Mateu Casañas met in that kitchen; their Disfrutar, open in Barcelona since December 2014, holds three Michelin stars and was No.1 on The World's 50 Best list in 2024.

The lineage is alive and eating: the Bullinianos, the foundation's community of former team members; Disfrutar, where three of them cook the No.1 restaurant of 2024 in the city where the workshop stood. The keeper's own words on the last night, as the BBC reported them on 30 July 2011: “El Bulli is not closing, it is transforming itself, because its soul is going to remain.”

Techniques & Recipes

The Method

How the kitchen actually worked: the empty months, the workshop, the numbered catalogue — and the one chemistry that made the sphere.

The method was a calendar before it was a technique. In 1987, at an Escoffier Foundation demonstration in Cannes, Adrià heard Jacques Maximin answer a question about creativity with one sentence, creativity means not copying, and the house dates its turn from re-creation to creation to that year. The same year the winter closure was extended to five months, mid-October to mid-March, for a reason the house still gives plainly: “we had very few customers in that period, and so keeping the restaurant open meant a considerable drain on the cash flow” (elBulli, History 1987–1993). The empty months became the work.

In 1994 the house formed what it called a development squad, a team devoted to creativity, and aimed it “not so much towards mixtures of products or variations on concepts that already existed … but to create new concepts and techniques” (elBulli, History 1994–1997); the foams and the frozen savoury courses came out of that decision. In 1997 the workshop, elBullitaller, was incubated; in 2000 it moved to Carrer Portaferrissa 7 in Barcelona; in 2001 the restaurant stopped serving lunch. The menu grew with the method: 27 courses at €115 in 2002 (The Guardian), 34 by 2010 (Vanity Fair), about forty in the last season (BBC).

The cataloguing started in 1999; work on the General Catalogue began in 2000; its first part was published in 2002 (elBulli, History 1998–2002). By the close the count stood at 1,846 “final tasting elaborations — the dishes that were placed before the diner,” and twenty-eight of them are rebuilt in the museum's kitchen (elBullifoundation press kit, 2023). A numbered record is what makes never repeat checkable.

Copy the technique without the calendar and it breaks in a predictable place.

The sphere lifted off a photograph and made in service. In the basic bath the skin keeps setting for as long as the bead sits in calcium, so the bead is served the minute it is lifted or it is a gummy pearl; and the fastest bath is the bitter one. Lee and Rogers timed gel strength reaching its plateau in about 100 seconds in calcium chloride, 500 in calcium lactate and 2,000 in calcium gluconate, and called chloride “the best option when the bitter taste can be masked” (Int. J. Gastronomy and Food Science, 2012 — a smaller journal).

That failure is not superstition — the technique is encoding a mechanism.

Sodium alginate is a chain from brown seaweed; a calcium ion carries two positive charges and slots between two chains at once, locking them together like eggs in a carton — the egg-box model of 1973 (Grant et al., FEBS Letters). Where alginate liquid meets a calcium bath a skin forms at the boundary and the inside stays liquid. Reverse the arrangement — calcium inside, alginate in the bath — and the gel can only form on the outside and stops there: the reverse sphere holds for hours, the basic one for minutes. The workshop's other trick runs against every jelly a cook knows: methylcellulose is liquid cold and sets when heated (Sarkar, J. Applied Polymer Science, 1979); and the house records noticing in 1998 that agar “could withstand high temperatures” — a jelly served warm.

Strip the chemistry away and one line of the tradition remains.

Creativity means not copying.

This coast is not the only school, and the other one fought back.

Sant Celoni against Cala Montjoi. Santi Santamaria of Can Fabes, the first Catalan chef to hold three stars (1994), used his 2008 book La cocina al desnudo to accuse the avant-garde, Adrià first, of feeding people harmful chemical substances, methylcellulose named; some eight hundred chefs answered with a manifesto accusing him of “una alarma social de consecuencias incalculables,” Adrià replied that his cooking rested on natural produce, and the health authorities found no danger (El País, 25 May 2008; The New York Times, 1 June 2008). Same coast, opposite creeds: the product first, or the technique and the concept first.

Why It Endures

Closed at No.1 on purpose — and what the closing protected.

The restaurant lost money for its last decade. In December 2006 Adrià told John Carlin it had run at a loss for six years, since the decision to drop lunch, and could “make four, five million euros profit a year” if it chose to (The Observer). It chose not to. “For us, the most important thing was creativity. So we decided to close for lunch, and the level of creativity kept getting higher. But at some point I realized we wouldn't be able to continue to evolve as a restaurant,” he told Vanity Fair in October 2010, the year the closure was announced, with the promise of what came next: “No Michelin, no customers, no pressure.”

The foundation was established on 7 February 2013, promoted by Adrià and Soler. On 15 June 2023 the building reopened as elBulli1846, “the first restaurant in the world to be turned into a museum”: almost four thousand square metres, sixty-nine installations, the dining room laid for a service that will not come, a four-hour film of dinner service, and a kitchen showing twenty-eight of the 1,846. It runs as a museum six months a year and as the foundation's laboratory, elBulliDNA, the rest. Adrià, in the opening release: “Will it be possible to eat here? No, otherwise it would be a restaurant. Our motto is: Eating knowledge.”

“The year 1846 saw the birth of August Escoffier, the father of modern cuisine. 1,846 was also the number of dishes created at elBullirestaurant before it finally closed its doors” — the foundation's own press kit, 2023. The most disruptive kitchen of its age named its monument after the most classical cook of the previous one, and after its own logbook.

What endured is hard to see because it won. The hydrocolloids the workshop learned to handle are ordinary professional kit; the habit behind them — understand a subject completely before you try to add to it — is now the foundation's Sapiens project and its Bullipedia, which we take apart in the companion piece, The Sapiens Method. The cost of the shortcut is the reverse: a kitchen that copies the sphere without the calendar gets the texture and none of the thinking.

On Board

On Board

From the cove to four square metres at anchor — what a galley can keep, sourced in the record.

Keep a catalogue. elBulli began numbering its dishes in 1999 and closed on 1,846; number yours — every dish you send on charter, with the date, what broke and what the guest said. Cook the family meal like a service. The Family Meal (Phaidon, 2011) is nothing but “the dishes that the staff at elBulli eats every day,” planned as menus; the crew mess is the same discipline. Choose your calcium. For a citrus or olive-brine pearl made at anchorage, make it a reverse sphere — calcium inside, alginate bath — so it holds for hours, and use calcium lactate or gluconate where chloride's bitterness cannot be rinsed (Lee & Rogers, 2012). Protect an hour. You cannot close five months; hold one hour a week off service for the dish you are still getting wrong.

Put the pen down, and the whole tradition comes down to one thing.

elBulli's real invention was not the foam; it was the empty months — protected time to think, and a numbered record of what the thinking produced. Keep the logbook, hold the hour, and never copy what you have not first understood. Tested at sea.

Every date and every degree above has a record behind it. Where the records disagree, it is printed here: the foundation's chronology gives the last service as 30 June 2011; contemporaneous reporting (BBC News, 30 July 2011) and every other record say 30 July.
