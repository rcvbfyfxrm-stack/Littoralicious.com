Taormina is a balcony — the view composed by a Greek theatre, and a wine country forty minutes behind it on Etna's north slope. Make or break: Taormina is two towns, before ten in the morning and after the day visitors leave; see it then, and spend the middle of the day in the vineyards between Castiglione and Randazzo. The soul of Taormina — the essay
Goethe's view, the visitors who invented the town, the wine country behind it and the strait to the north
THE ESSAY

On 7 May 1787 Goethe climbed to the top of Taormina's theatre and wrote that no audience had ever had such a scene before it: castles on rocks to the right, the town far below, and at the back Etna and the long chain of the coast to Catania. The view has not moved. The theatre was Greek-founded and Roman-rebuilt, and it was laid out so that the bay and the volcano are the backdrop.

The town that the visitors made. A German baron's photographs, an English gardener's follies on the cliff, D. H. Lawrence meeting a snake at his water trough: Taormina was invented as a destination by the people who came to stay, and Lady Florence Trevelyan bought the little island below it, Isola Bella, in 1890. Its one main street, Corso Umberto, runs from Porta Messina to Porta Catania with a terrace in the middle.

The north slope is the other reason to come. Forty minutes inland, between Linguaglossa, Castiglione di Sicilia and Randazzo, Etna wine was reborn: Nerello Mascalese on lava terraces, bottled by the contrada — the named lava parcel — and pressed, in the old houses, in stone palmenti. Randazzo is a medieval town built of black lava that the flows have never overrun; its trattorie cook mushrooms and the mountain's meat.

And the strait. North of Taormina the Ionian narrows into the Strait of Messina, whose opposing currents became Scylla and Charybdis, and where the swordfish boats still hunt from a tall mast and a long gangway. Come in October, when the day visitors thin, the sea is still warm, and the harvest is on the slope.

The theatre

Teatro Antico, TaorminaSignatureA 109 m bowl cut into the ridge, and a broken stage wall that turns Etna into the set.

With a cavea 109 m across, this is the second-largest ancient theatre in Sicily after Siracusa's, according to the park that runs it. The first theatre was Greek, of the 3rd century BC; three inscribed seats and a wall of squared blocks survive from it. Almost everything you see is Roman, rebuilt in the first half of the 2nd century AD under Trajan or Hadrian, and the stage front was partly reassembled in the 19th century. The central doorway has fallen; the gap left is the frame for the volcano. Sit high on the north side, then look the other way: the Calabrian coast and the Strait.

Come at 09:00 opening and climb to the top rim before the coach groups arrive.

Going up, honestly

The summit rules, as of October 2026Read before you goAbove about 2,500 m the volcano is a regulated zone; since 14 August 2026 its inner ring is shut to hikers.

Etna's summit is no longer open hillside. In August 2025 Sicily's civil protection adopted INGV-drawn zones: a Summit Zone (the craters, upper Valle del Bove to 1,700 m, and roughly everything above 2,500 m on the south, west and north) and inside it a Permanent Hazard Zone around the active craters. On 13-14 August 2026, with lava fountains judged imminent, the summit towns suspended all excursions in that inner zone until further notice; licensed alpine and volcanological guides, CAI staff and scientists are exempt. On 24 September 2026 Etna erupted again, with a 4 km ash column. Treat any summit sale as conditional.

Check the INGV bulletin and the Parco dell'Etna ordinances the evening before, every time.

Etna birchVolcano gemA birch left behind by the Ice Age, growing nowhere on earth but this volcano.

Betula aetnensis grows only on Etna, between about 1,200 and 2,000 m. It is an Ice Age relic: when the cold retreated and birches moved north, this population stayed on the mountain and became its own species. It is often the first tree to take hold on old lava, so its white trunks stand out against black rock. The best stands are on the north side above Linguaglossa, around Piano Provenzana.

Look for white trunks on black lava above 1,200 m on the Mareneve road.

True stories

Scylla, Charybdis and the remaSea storyTwo seas, tides out of step, five knots over a sill: the monster was a tidal gate.

Homer put a six-headed monster on the Calabrian side and a swallowing whirlpool, Charybdis, near Capo Peloro. Behind the myth: a strait 32 km long and 3 km wide at its narrowest, joining two seas whose tides are out of step. When the Tyrrhenian is high the Ionian is low, and water rushes across a shallow sill. Pilots call the stream the rema: the scendente runs north to south, the montante south to north, each about six hours, with slack water between. It reaches about 5 knots, throws off eddies and internal waves seen from satellites, and the montante lifts deep Ionian water, and its lanternfish and hatchetfish, onto the beaches near Ganzirri and Capo Peloro, recorded since at least 1909.

★ The old hand's point: Treat the strait as a tidal gate: time it for slack or a fair stream, stay out of the ferry lanes and the separation scheme, and expect overfalls off the points even in calm weather.

Touch it today
Capo Peloro / Torre Faro at dawn after a winter Scirocco with the montante running: stranded deep-sea fish, and swirls visible off the point

The felucaSea storyA mast to see the fish and a gangway longer than the boat to reach it before it hears you.

Each spring and summer swordfish pass through the Strait of Messina, and a boat built for nothing else goes to meet them: the feluca. It carries a tall lattice lookout mast and a passerella, a gangway about a metre wide that reaches forward roughly one and a half times the boat's length. Lookouts at the masthead spot a fish basking near the surface; the helmsman drives up from astern, and the harpooner, standing at the very tip of the gangway, strikes before the hull is close enough to scare it. Ganzirri is the Sicilian base, Scilla and Bagnara the Calabrian. On 26 March 2026 the Region announced nearly EUR 386,000 of EU money to keep the feluche working, calling them a selective system that drastically reduces unwanted catch.

★ The old hand's point: Height of eye is everything: the mast solves seeing through glitter, the gangway solves the bow wave. Every lookout and every helmsman who has picked up a buoy upwind knows both problems.

Touch it today
Ganzirri and Torre Faro, north of Messina, where feluche lie along the shore in season (roughly late spring to late summer); eat swordfish alla messinese or swordfish involtini

The Russian sailors of Messina, 1908Sea gem05:20, 28 December 1908: Russian warships at Augusta steam north into a destroyed city.

At 05:20 on 28 December 1908 a magnitude 7.1 earthquake and a tsunami up to 12 m destroyed Messina and Reggio; around 120,000 died. The Russian training squadron of Rear-Admiral Vladimir Litvinov was lying at Augusta. The battleships Tsesarevich and Slava and the cruisers Admiral Makarov and Bogatyr steamed for Messina and their sailors were among the first rescuers in the ruins; British ships including HMS Exmouth followed. The city waited more than a century to say thank you in bronze: the monument designed by Pietro Kufferle in 1911 was only installed in 2012.

A seaman's story: the first help came from ships, not from land.

Where to swim

Isola BellaTaorminaA king's gift, an Englishwoman's garden, rooms cut in the rock, and a pebble bar the sea crosses.

Isola Bella's paper trail reads like a novel. In 1806 Ferdinand I of Bourbon gave the rock to Taormina's mayor, Pancrazio Ciprioti; in 1890 Florence Trevelyan bought it, named it Isolabella and planted an English-style garden into the native scrub. The Bosurgi family, Messina industrialists who owned it from 1938, cut small dwellings into the rock among the plants. The Region bought it and made it a nature reserve in 1998. It reopened on 20 June 2026 after months of closure: you walk the pebble bar from Mazzaro, then climb through terraces, vaulted passages and stairways.

Go for the rock-cut house and the garden, not the sunbed.

Taormina to Castelmola on the old pathWalkStone steps from the town wall to a 16th-century church carved into the cliff, then the castle.

This is the old way up, before the road. From the upper edge of Taormina the path climbs in switchbacks of stone and packed earth to the Madonna della Rocca, a 16th-century chapel built and carved into the rock: inside, the ceiling is bare cliff, the walls painted with delicate flowers. A long staircase cut into the rock continues to the Saracen castle on Monte Tauro, the 'upper' acropolis taken by the Normans in 1079 after they ringed the town with 22 wooden towers. Then on to Castelmola. The views fall straight onto the Teatro Antico and Isola Bella.

One of the few places in Taormina where you can be alone, and the castle is open again.

Corso Umberto, TaorminaTaorminaOne street on a Roman road, a parliament palace of 1411, and a terrace hung over the sea.

Walk it twice: before 09:30 for the stone, after 21:00 for the town.

Villa Comunale and Lady Florence TrevelyanGardenAn exiled Englishwoman's cliff garden, with brick-and-lava follies she built to watch birds.

Florence Trevelyan, from Newcastle and said to have been sent abroad after a romance with the future Edward VII, settled in Taormina in the 1880s and married the town doctor, Salvatore Cacciola, in 1890. On terraces above the sea she laid out an English garden and built Victorian follies of brick and lava stone, the 'beehives', where she sat to watch birds. She died in 1907 and the garden passed to the town. Today the Villa Comunale is the calmest public space in Taormina, and the lower terrace looks down the coast to Giardini Naxos.

The quietest hour in Taormina is a morning on her terraces.

The visitors

Wilhelm von GloedenArtA German baron, a camera and the agaves: the photographs that sold Taormina to Europe.

Wilhelm von Gloeden came to Taormina in 1878 for his health and stayed almost until his death in 1931. He photographed local young men posed as figures of Greek antiquity among the ruins and agaves, and the prints travelled across Europe, drawing Oscar Wilde, Kaiser Wilhelm II and the Krupps. The work is uncomfortable today for its homoerotic and exploitative side, and part of the archive was destroyed by the Fascist police in the 1930s. What survives hangs in some hotels and bars.

How to read it

Reading a contrada on the labelContradeSame grape, same cellar, different lava flow: the contrada is Etna's cru.

A contrada is a named district of the volcano, often a single lava flow of known date. A producer may print it on an Etna Rosso or Bianco label: Calderara Sottana, Santo Spirito, Feudo di Mezzo, Guardiola, Rampante, Arcuria. The count depends on who you ask: Decanter reports 133 contrade; Jamie Goode counts 142 units across 11 communes (2024). Vinous credits Marc de Grazia of Tenuta delle Terre Nere with making the contrada Etna's language, and adds that from the 2026 vintage communal names may also appear on front labels.

Taste two contrade side by side from the same producer and vintage; the difference is the lesson.

The palmento: a winery made of stone and gravityPalmentoA stone press-house worked by gravity, banned for winemaking in 1997, still on every old farm.

A palmento is a stone wine-press building set into a slope. Grapes were trodden in an upper basin, the must ran by gravity into a lava-stone vat below, and a great wooden beam press finished the pomace. Jamie Goode writes that EU hygiene rules ended palmento winemaking in 1997, pushing growers to sell grapes instead of wine; some still use them quietly, and Salvo Foti has exported palmento wine labelled experimental. You can stand inside restored ones at Graci, at Palmento Costanzo, where it is still the working cellar, and at Zash in Riposto, where a starred dining room fills the old fermenting vat.

Ask to see the press beam: it is the whole machine in one timber.

Worth the drive

Gole dell'AlcantaraNatureLava cracked into basalt organ pipes, a river that stays cold in August, and a EUR 2 staircase.

An old Etna lava flow filled the bed of the Alcantara and, as it cooled, split into hexagonal basalt columns. The river then cut back through it, leaving a narrow canyon whose walls rise like organ pipes to about 25 m. The name is Arabic, al-qantara, 'the bridge', after a Roman bridge that stood here. The water stays cold even in high summer. You choose your way in: the municipal stairs, about 200 steps down to the river beach, or the private Parco Botanico e Geologico with its lift.

Take the municipal stairs early, before the private park's groups, and wade if the river allows.

Savoca and Forza d'AgroThe GodfatherCoppola could not shoot in Corleone, so Michael asked for Apollonia's hand here.

Francis Ford Coppola could not film in Corleone itself and found his Sicily in these two Peloritani hill villages north of Taormina. In Savoca, Bar Vitelli in Palazzo Trimarchi is where Michael asks Apollonia's father for her hand, and the church of San Nicolo (Santa Lucia) is where they marry. Savoca also keeps a Capuchin crypt of mummified 18th-century notables. Forza d'Agro, on the next ridge, gave other scenes. Both look across the water to Calabria.

Go early or late: Savoca fills with tour groups 11:00-16:00.

Castiglione di SiciliaEtna NorthA sandstone spur over the Alcantara, and a Byzantine church standing alone among vines.

Castiglione di Sicilia sits on a sandstone spur above the Alcantara valley, with a ruined castle and a cluster of churches. Its land runs up Etna's north flank and takes in Passopisciaro, Solicchiata and Verzella, where Nerello Mascalese grows on old lava terraces: half the famous north-slope names are in this one commune. Below the village, the Cuba di Santa Domenica, a small Byzantine church of the 7th-9th century, stands by itself in the vineyards.

In October you arrive in the middle of harvest; pair the village with one winery.

RandazzoEtna NorthThe town closest to the craters, built of black lava, and never once entered by a flow.

Randazzo lies about 15 km from the summit craters, the closest town to them, yet lava has never come inside it; locals credit their patron. Its churches of Santa Maria, San Martino and San Nicolo, and its walls, are built of dark lava stone. It was a royal seat under the Aragonese. In 1943 the German army held a line here and the town was heavily bombed; much is rebuilt. The Circumetnea railway stops here on its slow loop around the volcano.

Come for the black stone and stay for a granita at Musumeci on Piazza Santa Maria.

Before you go

3 to know

The warnings

Corso Umberto at midday, and Isola Bella sunbeds in Augustthe warningTaormina is two towns: the one before 10:00, and the one the cruise ships bring.

From late morning to late afternoon in season, Corso Umberto and the Teatro Antico take thousands of cruise and coach day visitors, and terraces on the main street charge for the view rather than the food. At Isola Bella in July and August the free beach is tiny and lidos price sunbeds to match. The fix is timing, not avoidance: sleep in town, walk the Corso early, be at the theatre gate at opening, eat in the side streets or up in Castelmola.

Same town, different hour: you get it back before 10:00.

Avoid
Corso Umberto 10:00-18:00 in season; Isola Bella sunbeds in Jul-Aug
Instead
Theatre at opening, dinner in the side alleys or Castelmola
Driving into Ortigia and Taorminathe warningThe camera at the gate photographs your plate; the fine finds you months later.

Ortigia and central Taormina are ZTL zones, limited-traffic areas watched by cameras. The fine arrives months later, often through the rental company with its own fee on top, and satellite navigation will happily route you straight in. Park outside and walk: the Talete car park for Ortigia, Lumbi or Porta Catania for Taormina. Both old towns are small enough that the walk in is shorter than the hunt for a legal space inside.

If your hotel is inside the ZTL, ask it to register your plate before you arrive.

Where
Ortigia (Siracusa) and central Taormina
Park
Talete (Ortigia); Lumbi or Porta Catania (Taormina)
Tip
Hotel registers your plate in advance
Etna 'summit' tours sold from Taorminathe warning'Summit' often means the Silvestri craters and the souvenir shops at 1,900 m.

Many day tours sold on Corso Umberto or at the cruise terminal drive to Rifugio Sapienza, give you an hour among the Crateri Silvestri, which are free and easy to walk alone, and the shops, then drive back. It can be a pleasant day, but it is not the summit, and with the inner hazard zone shut to hikers since 14 August 2026, any promise of 'reaching the craters' should be checked against the ordinance in force.

Book a licensed volcanological guide directly and ask three questions before paying.

Who knows

Read before you go: Verga, Vittorini, Quasimodo, BrancatiReadFour writers born on this coast, and one house in Aci Trezza that a novel made real.

Giovanni Verga (Catania, 1840) wrote I Malavoglia (1881), a fishing family in Aci Trezza fighting the sea and debt, and the stories of Cavalleria rusticana. Elio Vittorini (Siracusa, 1908) wrote Conversazione in Sicilia (1941), a son's journey home across the island. Salvatore Quasimodo (Modica, 1901, Nobel 1959) wrote the Anapo and Siracusa into verse. Vitaliano Brancati (Pachino, 1907) mocked Catanese manners in Il bell'Antonio. For a sailor, start with I Malavoglia.

I Malavoglia is the fishing coast you will anchor off.

Through the year

Etna harvest, OctoberOctoberOn the volcano, Nerello Mascalese is often the last grape picked in Italy.

Etna's vineyards climb past 1,000 m, and at that height Nerello Mascalese ripens slowly: it is often picked in the second half of October, weeks after the coast has finished. The Etna DOC, created in 1968, is one of Sicily's oldest. In October you see crews on the steep terraces of loose lava, crates stacked by old stone palmenti that still stand in many vineyards. Some wineries open harvest days; ask directly.

Late October on the north side is the one time the volcano smells of crushed grapes.

When
Second half of October, at altitude
Where
North side: Castiglione, Randazzo, Linguaglossa

Le Contrade dell'Etna, the growers' fair started in 2009, held its 17th edition on 19-20 April 2026 at the Sikania Garden Village in Randazzo, with tastings and master classes. The Consorzio also runs Etna Days, a September preview for press and trade in Castiglione di Sicilia. For a chef building a wine list for the season, the April fair puts dozens of producers at one table for a day.

Seasonal
What each part of the year is for around Taormina — the blood orange, the tragedies, the granita and the harvest
4 seasons
October to DecemberOctober: the Etna harvest finishes, the Ottobrata fills Zafferana on Sundays, and the sea is still warm enough to swim (around 23 C). Eat: porcini and other mushrooms from the Etna woods, chestnuts, prickly pears, fresh mostarda (grape-must jelly), new olive oil from late October. November: San Martino (11 Nov) opens the new wine with roast chestnuts; first rains. December: Santa Lucia (13 Dec) in Siracusa, cuccìa instead of bread; Christmas with buccellati (fig-filled pastries) and presepi in the churches.
January to MarchJanuary and February are the season of the blood orange from the plain of Catania (Arancia Rossa di Sicilia IGP; the cold nights near Etna develop the red pigment). Sant'Agata (3-5 Feb) brings minne and olivette. Almond trees blossom in February. Carnival in Acireale is one of the island's oldest, with papier-mache and flower floats. In March, wild fennel and asparagus; the INDA box office is busy before the May season. Etna often has snow and skiing above Nicolosi and Linguaglossa.
April to JuneEaster brings processions in every town. INDA tragedies run in Siracusa from April or May to late June (2027: from 7 May). Noto's Infiorata is held mid-May. Fresh broad beans, artichokes, and wild greens fill the markets; ricotta is at its best in spring. In May-June the swordfish season begins in the Strait of Messina and the bluefin tuna pass. The sea warms to swimming temperature by June.
July to SeptemberSummer means granita and brioche for breakfast, concerts in Taormina's Teatro Antico, and long evenings in the piazzas. Sant'Agata's summer feast is on 17 August. Etna summer paroxysms can bring ash and crowds to the slopes (2026 brought both). Late summer: the grape harvest begins on the coast, figs and prickly pears ripen, and in odd years Bronte harvests pistachios (late August-September). The Ortigia Film Festival is in late September.

The Dish — where the list comes from

The stories behind the canon, and where they are argued rather than settled.

#1
DISH

Etna Rosso and the contrada

A contrada is a named parcel of land, on Etna often a single lava flow, and since the north slope's rebirth the best houses bottle each one separately: same grape, same cellar, a different lava. The idea is Burgundy's climat, and it is why an Etna label can name a place smaller than a village.
WhereTenuta delle Terre Nere, Randazzo · Graci, Castiglione · Maps →
#2
DISH

The palmento

Every old Etna farmhouse had one: a stone winery where grapes were trodden in an upper basin and the must ran by gravity into vats below. EU hygiene rules have barred making wine in them for sale since 1997; a few houses restored theirs, to visit or — at Zash — to dine in.
