ISTANBUL — WHAT TO EAT & WHAT TO DRINK
The Terroir checklist · littoralicious.com/terroir/Istanbul-Bogazici
★ = le préféré du chef

 1. Simit ★  (say: see-MEET)
    The sesame bread ring, dipped in grape molasses before the seeds go on — that is the dark lacquer.
    Why: Ottoman price registers had it in Istanbul by 1525; the state fixed its weight by decree because too many people lived off it.
    Best place: Off the tray on the Eminönü–Kadıköy vapur
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Emin%C3%B6n%C3%BC%20Ferry%20Pier%20Istanbul

 2. Serpme kahvaltı ★  (say: SERP-meh kah-vahl-TUH)
    The sprawled Sunday breakfast — twenty small plates, three hours, tables held rather than turned. You get to taste everything at once.
    Why: Kahvaltı means "before coffee": the biggest meal of the Turkish day is named for the drink it precedes.
    Best place: Van Kahvaltı Evi, Cihangir — Sunday, go early
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Van%20Kahvalt%C4%B1%20Evi%20Cihangir%20Istanbul

 3. Muhammara ★  (say: moo-hahm-mah-RAH)
    Walnuts, red-pepper paste and pomegranate molasses pounded into a coarse paste — on the breakfast spread and the meze tray both.
    Why: The Hatay–Aleppo table in one bowl: the south-eastern half of the city's cooking, spread on bread before nine in the morning.
    Best place: On the serpme spread, or at an Antakya house — Hatay Medeniyetler Sofrası
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hatay%20Medeniyetler%20Sofras%C4%B1%20Istanbul

 4. Menemen ★  (say: meh-neh-MEN)
    Eggs scrambled loosely into tomato and green pepper, eaten from the pan with bread — the Turkish cousin of shakshuka.
    Why: Turkey's great breakfast argument: with onion or without (soğanlı / soğansız) genuinely divides the country. Order it and take a side.
    Best place: Çakmak Kahvaltı Salonu, Beşiktaş
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%87akmak%20Kahvalt%C4%B1%20Salonu%20Be%C5%9Fikta%C5%9F%20Istanbul

 5. Yaprak sarma ★  (say: yah-PRAHK sahr-MAH)
    Vine leaves rolled thin as a finger around rice, herbs and spice, eaten cold with a squeeze of lemon. The good ones, mind — hand-rolled, tight, no shortcuts.
    Why: The zeytinyağlı register — the palace half of the table: vegetables cooked in olive oil and served cold, restraint as a cuisine.
    Best place: The zeytinyağlı trays at Çiya Sofrası, Kadıköy
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%87iya%20Sofras%C4%B1%20Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

 6. Mercimek çorbası ★  (say: mehr-jee-MEK chor-bah-SUH)
    Red lentil soup — and the ritual matters: fresh lemon squeezed in, pul biber on top. Never skip either.
    Why: The most democratic dish in Turkey: every lokanta opens with it, every taxi driver has an opinion on it, and it costs almost nothing.
    Best place: Yanyalı Fehmi Lokantası, Kadıköy — trading since 1919
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Yanyal%C4%B1%20Fehmi%20Lokantas%C4%B1%20Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

 7. Çiğ köfte ★  (say: CHEE kuf-TEH)
    Fine bulgur kneaded for hours with pepper paste and a dozen spices, wrapped in lettuce with lemon — a "tartare" of grain.
    Why: The name means raw meatball and it no longer contains any: the street version went meatless by regulation, and the knead survived the meat.
    Best place: Bezirgan Çiğ Köfte Salonu, Kadıköy — call ahead
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bezirgan%20%C3%87i%C4%9F%20K%C3%B6fte%20Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

 8. Balık ekmek ★  (say: bah-LUHK ek-MEK)
    Grilled fish in a half loaf, eaten standing by the water.
    Why: No inventor and no founding date — it is what happens when a fishing fleet ties up at the foot of a hungry city.
    Best place: The Karaköy waterfront counters — not the painted Eminönü boats
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=bal%C4%B1k%20ekmek%20Karak%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

 9. Turşu — the pickles ★  (say: toor-SHOO)
    Cucumber, cabbage, beet, green plum — pickled sharp, sold from barrels, the brine drunk by the glass as turşu suyu.
    Why: The turşucu is a shop that sells nothing else, and has not needed to for a century. Drink the juice: it is the point.
    Best place: A turşucu in the Kadıköy çarşı
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=tur%C5%9Fucu%20Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

10. Ayran ★  (say: eye-RAHN)
    Salted yoghurt whipped thin, best from the churn (yayık) with a collar of foam.
    Why: A confession: the house hates it. It is still a must — no kebab is complete without the glass beside it, and Turkey drinks more of it than anything else.
    Best place: From the yayık wherever you sit at a fire — Tam Ocakbaşı
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tam%20Ocakba%C5%9F%C4%B1%20Arnavutk%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

11. Midye dolma  (say: MEED-yeh DOHL-mah)
    Mussels stuffed with spiced rice, sold from trays after dark, one lemon squeeze per shell.
    Why: An Armenian dish before it was an Istanbul one — recorded in an 1888 yearbook, made with large mussels and sheep-tail fat.
    Best place: Midyeci Ahmet, Karaköy
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Midyeci%20Ahmet%20Karak%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

12. Ciğer at the ocakbaşı  (say: jee-EHR at the oh-jak-bah-SHUH)
    Liver first, then the skewers, a lahmacun to close — you sit at the charcoal trench and the cook works in front of you.
    Why: The most Istanbul way to eat fire: the ocakbaşı is a bar with a grill in it, carried north by south-eastern migration.
    Best place: Tam Ocakbaşı, Arnavutköy — book for Friday or Saturday
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tam%20Ocakba%C5%9F%C4%B1%20Arnavutk%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

13. Fıstıklı kuru baklava  (say: fuhs-tuhk-LUH koo-ROO bahk-lah-VAH)
    The dry pistachio baklava — no cream, cut small, bought by weight.
    Why: The Güllü family carried the craft from Damascus to Gaziantep in 1871; the dry version survives being carried onto a boat.
    Best place: Karaköy Güllüoğlu — open past midnight
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Karak%C3%B6y%20G%C3%BCll%C3%BCo%C4%9Flu%20Istanbul

14. Künefe  (say: kew-neh-FEH)
    Shredded pastry over molten cheese, cooked to order and eaten hot — it does not wait for you.
    Why: Antakya's dessert, made properly in Istanbul by houses that came north from Hatay.
    Best place: Hatay Medeniyetler Sofrası, off İstiklal
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hatay%20Medeniyetler%20Sofras%C4%B1%20Istanbul

15. Boza  (say: BOH-zah)
    Fermented millet, thick and faintly sour, with cinnamon and roasted chickpeas on top. Winter only — it spoils in heat.
    Why: Vefa Bozacısı has poured it since 1876 in a room that has barely changed; the only shop of its generation still trading.
    Best place: Vefa Bozacısı, Fatih
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Vefa%20Bozac%C4%B1s%C4%B1%20Istanbul

16. Türk kahvesi  (say: tewrk kah-veh-SEE)
    Coffee milled finer than espresso, brought up slowly in the cezve and never allowed to boil — judge it by the foam.
    Why: The word kahvaltı itself bows to this cup: everything eaten before it is just preparation.
    Best place: Fazıl Bey, Kadıköy çarşı
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Faz%C4%B1l%20Bey%20T%C3%BCrk%20Kahvesi%20Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y%20Istanbul

17. Çay on the ferry deck  (say: CHAI)
    Tea in a tulip glass from the tray that never spills, on the open deck mid-strait.
    Why: Twenty minutes, the price of a bus ride, and the best view in the city is included.
    Best place: Any Şehir Hatları vapur — take the aft deck
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Emin%C3%B6n%C3%BC%20Ferry%20Pier%20Istanbul

18. Rakı-balık  (say: rah-KUH bah-LUHK)
    The full meyhane night: cold mezes, then hot, then one fish — chosen off the ice by the door and weighed in front of you, never off a menu.
    Why: The meyhane table was built by the city's Greeks, Armenians and Jews; the ritual outlived its authors.
    Best place: Agora Meyhanesi 1890, Balat
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Agora%20Meyhanesi%201890%20Istanbul

19. Tavuk göğsü  (say: tah-VOOK goess-SUE)
    A milk pudding thickened with chicken breast pulled so fine you cannot find it — sweet, not savoury.
    Why: A palace dessert a patisserie cannot make; order it where a muhallebici still exists to make it.
    Best place: Saray Muhallebicisi, İstiklal
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Saray%20Muhallebicisi%20%C4%B0stiklal%20Istanbul

20. İşkembe soup  (say: eesh-kem-BEH)
    Tripe soup, garlic vinegar on top, taken after midnight against the rakı.
    Why: The city's licensed cure: işkembeci keep the hours the meyhane makes necessary.
    Best place: Lale İşkembecisi, Tarlabaşı — since 1965
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lale%20%C4%B0%C5%9Fkembecisi%20Istanbul

21. Öküzgözü  (say: oh-kooz-goh-ZOO)
    The juicy, dark-fruited Anatolian red — the name means "ox eye", after the size of the berries.
    Why: One of the indigenous grapes almost nobody outside Turkey orders — which is exactly why to order it here.
    Best place: Solera Winery, Tomtom
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Solera%20Winery%20Istanbul

22. Boğazkere  (say: boh-ahz-keh-REH)
    The tannic Anatolian red — the name means "throat burner". Built for lamb off the fire.
    Why: Drink it at an ocakbaşı and the pairing explains both the grape and the grill.
    Best place: Sensus Wine Boutique, Galata
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sensus%20Wine%20Boutique%20Istanbul

23. Narince & Emir  (say: nah-rin-DJEH · eh-MEER)
    The Anatolian whites, cold: Narince from Tokat, Emir from Cappadocia.
    Why: Grown on some of the oldest wine ground on earth, and still barely exported.
    Best place: WAYANA Wine Bar, Karaköy — natural list
    Map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=WAYANA%20Wine%20Bar%20Istanbul
