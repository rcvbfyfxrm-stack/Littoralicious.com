[history]
History & Terroir Where it comes from Brač stone → White House Diocletian's Palace (305 AD) was built in Brač white stone — the same that later went to the White House in Washington (west facade, 1791) and the Reichstag in Berlin (lower sections). This stone, kamen Brač , defines Dalmatia: poor, draining, limestone soils — perfect for vine and olive but not for wheat. Hence the cuisine — radically maritime and mineral. The region was Venetian from 1420 to 1797 (six hundred years), which explains brodet (cousin of brodetto ), pašticada (braised beef descended from stracotto ), the pastas ( fuži , makaruni ). Under Maria Theresa and then the Habsburgs until 1918, Viennese coffee and pastry took hold. Korčula Pošip (a mineral, indigenous, native white documented since the 15th century) and Plavac Mali ("little blue") red — identified in 2001 by UC Davis DNA analysis as the Dalmatian ancestor of California Zinfandel — are the two pillar varieties.

[eat]
What to Eat At table peka → order yesterday Three pillars. Peka ("under the bell") — meat or octopus + potatoes + rosemary under a cast-iron bell covered with embers for 2–3 hours, to be ordered the day before — this is the Sunday meal, not a fast-service dish. Gregada — Hvar fish stew (scorpionfish, weever, sea bream) with potatoes, white wine, garlic, parsley, no tomato — the authentic Dalmatian, predating Venetian contact. Brodet — Venetian-influenced version, tomato + red wine + several fish, served on polenta. For meat: Brač lamb (indigenous breed raised in island salt-air, 18 months under Croatian AOP) as peka, and pašticada (beef marinated 24h in vinegar + Prošek, braised 4h in Plavac Mali, served on gnocchi). On charcuterie: pršut from Drniš (ham dried in the bora wind, AOP), paški sir (Pag sheep cheese, fed on salt-herb pastures).

[drink]
What to Drink By the glass Tribidrag, the true Zin Three varieties to know. Pošip from Korčula — mineral, dry white, candied citrus and green almond; the great Dalmatian white, IGP since 1967. Plavac Mali from Pelješac — dense red, round tannins, ripe plums; two reference cuvées: Dingač (45–65 % south-facing slopes, the most powerful) and Postup (more structured); UC Davis 2001 DNA: ancestor of Zinfandel and Primitivo, shared descent with Crljenak Kaštelanski ("Tribidrag"), found in the old Kaštela vineyards 15 km from Split. Tribidrag/Crljenak — precisely what the lab identified as the "true" Zinfandel, tasted on site at Zinfandel Food & Wine Bar . To explore: Paradox (the Croatian-wine reference of Split). Raisin-style local wine bars are the right place to discover the Dalmatian appellation map and meet a sommelier who pours small Croatian producers — see Paradox Wine & Cheese Bar and Zinfandel in the Drink sfold . Aperitif: Prošek (sweet straw-dried wine, not to be confused with Italian Prosecco). Eau-de-vie: rakija of fig, of mulberry, or travarica with karst herbs.

[walk]
A WALK The path Leave from the Golden Gate (north, marble, Meštrović's 8 m statue, 1929: Grgur Ninski, the bishop who imposed Slavonic), descend the Decumanus to the Peristyle — ceremonial heart of the palace, millennia-old red-porphyry Egyptian sphinx, Aswan granite columns. Turn right to the Mausoleum (became Saint Domnius in 650 — Domnius was the bishop Diocletian had beheaded in this same palace 350 years earlier; the irony is Dalmatian ). Climb the 13th-c. campanile for the Riva view. Down to the substructures — intact vaulted cellars, kebab providers for Game of Thrones . Out by the Bronze Gate south, take the Riva right, reach Matejuška at sunset. Chilled Pošip glass. One hour forty-five.

To extend beyond the centre: the inland and island konobe of Brač, Hvar and Vis are the natural next step — they justify a whole separate trip.

[dish]
The Dish Canonical dishes — the plates that define Dalmatia 8 dishes Peka is a cooking method, not a recipe. An iron (or terra-cotta) bell heated white on embers, set over meat or fish, covered with red ash — slow cooking (1h30 to 2h30), low temperature, sealed steam. Probably Illyrian in origin, predating Romanization. The best konobe still practice it over wood fire, in the open air or inner courtyards, by booking 24 hours ahead because the setup takes time.
  <card dish:🍖 Pašticada à la dalmate> Beef marinated 24h · Prošek · prunes, pork lard, rosemary — the Dalmatian Sunday dish A slice of top round of beef larded with pork fat, garlic and cloves, marinated 24 hours in wine vinegar with carrots, onions, rosemary. Braised 4 hours with dried prunes, Prošek wine (Dalmatian straw wine), tomato, sometimes Plavac Mali. Thick brown-leather sauce. Served with njoki (Croatian gnocchi, denser than Italian) that soak up the sauce. A feast dish — Sundays, weddings. Reference at Apetit et Fife .
  <card dish:🍲 Brodet (brujet, brodetto)> Dalmatian fish stew · tomato, white wine · polenta — cousin of bourride and caciucco Several fish (red mullet, grouper, monkfish, scorpionfish, sometimes cuttlefish or langoustine), onion, garlic, crushed tomato, parsley, Dalmatian white wine, Brač or Hvar olive oil, vinegar. 30 min uncovered to reduce. Served on white polenta (palenta) or on a slice of stale bread. Direct cousin of Provençal bourride and Italian Adriatic brodetto. At Dvor , at Konoba Marjan .
  <card dish:🦊 Peka — agneau / poulpe / veau sous la cloche> Heated iron bell · red ashes · 2h — book 24 hours ahead See the funfact above. Three canonical versions: janjetina ispod peke (Dalmatian milk-lamb, usually from Brač or Pag), hobotnica ispod peke (octopus), teletina ispod peke (veal). Potatoes, carrots, onions, rosemary, sometimes olive oil. The bell traps the vapors — the meat bathes in its own juices while caramelizing at the base. 24-hour booking required everywhere. At Matejuška (octopus peka), at Šperun (lamb peka, by booking), at Dvor (milk-lamb peka).
  <card dish:🍲 Crni rižot — squid-ink risotto> Black rice · cuttlefish · Dalmatian white wine — cousin of Venetian black risotto The cuttlefish is cooked with its own ink, which dyes the rice deep black. Arborio or Carnaroli rice, onion, garlic, white wine (Pošip or Maraština), finished with olive oil. All'onda texture like Venice — wet, not dry. Standard along the Dalmatian coast; in Split, one of the best versions is at Konoba Fetivi .
  <card dish:🍳 Soparnik> Thin dough wrapper · chard, onion, olive oil — Poljica specialty, Croatian PGI since 2016 Traditional dish of Poljica, 30 km south of Split. Two paper-thin dough sheets (flour, water, salt, oil), filling of chopped chard, spring onion, garlic, parsley, plenty of Dalmatian olive oil. Traditionally baked under the ashes of an open hearth. Croatian PGI (Specijalitet zaštićenog zemljopisnog podrijetla) since 2016. Medieval fasting dish — no meat, no cheese. Ask for it in the inland konobas.
  <card dish:🍮 Rožata> Crème caramel scented with rožalin — the Dalmatian crème caramel Traditional crème caramel from Dubrovnik and southern Dalmatia, scented with rožalin (rose-petal liqueur, once made in convents). Denser than French crème caramel, slightly curdled. Served cold. The best is in Dubrovnik (Konoba Lokanda Peskarija); several Split houses offer it.
  <card dish:🍲 Viška pogača> Vis island · anchovy, onion, tomato flatbread — the Adriatic's Venetian-style «pizza» Thin leavened dough, topped with melted onions, salted anchovies, tomatoes, sometimes capers, olive oil. Distant cousin of Naples pizza, but from a separate Adriatic tradition — pogača names a family of flatbreads across the Balkans. Vis-island specialty (sold hot from Komiža's oven). A few Split bakeries offer it on weekends — ask.
  <card dish:🥩 Pršut + Paški sir> Smoked Dalmatian ham + Pag sheep cheese — the standard apéritif platter Pršut — pork ham dried 12 to 18 months, exposed to the bura (cold, dry northeast wind), lightly smoked in certain terroirs (Drniš, Istria). Dry texture, salinity, fragrant fat. Direct cousin of jambon de Bayonne and Tyrol Speck. Paški sir — Pag-island sheep cheese, Croatian AOP. The sheep graze on grass constantly salted by the bura , giving the milk a unique mineral signature. Hard-pressed cheese, dark rind, six months minimum aging. The best are at 36 months, dry and caramelized.

[drink]
What to drink Plavac Mali, Pošip, Vugava, Tribidrag — and rakija at every hour 8 entries Zinfandel is Croatian. In 2001, UC Davis confirmed by DNA that American Zinfandel is genetically identical to Tribidrag (also called Crljenak Kaštelanski), cultivated in Dalmatia since the 15th century. The same variety is also called Primitivo in southern Italy. The proof was published by Carole Meredith of UC Davis — and it was in the vineyards around Kaštela (15 km from Split) that the last ancient vines were found.
  <card drink:🍷 Plavac Mali — le grand rouge dalmate> Pelješac · Dingač, Postup · descendant of Tribidrag — structured tannins, black cherry, garrigue Dalmatia's major red variety, a crossing descended from Tribidrag (Zinfandel) and Dobričić. Grown on steep slopes of the Pelješac peninsula (southern Dalmatia) — the two grand crus are Dingač (south-facing near-terraced vines, limestone, sea wind) and Postup (north-facing). Marked tannin, 14°+ alcohol, ripe black cherry, garrigue, sometimes animal. Producers: Grgić Vina (Mike Grgich, the Croat of Napa Valley who won the Judgement of Paris 1976, returned to make wine in Dalmatia), Saints Hills, Korta Katarina. Producers Grgić Vina · Saints Hills · Korta Katarina · Bura
  <card drink:🍷 Pošip — le grand blanc de Korčula> Korčula (island) · salinity, honey, apricot — Dalmatia's near-Burgundy Indigenous white grape from Korčula island, mostly grown at Smokvica and Čara in the island's center. Atlantic salinity despite the Adriatic, apricot, honey, sometimes wild fennel. Tank-fermented for fresh styles, barrel for top cuvées. Producers: Toreta, Bire, Krajančić, PZ Pošip Čara (cooperative). Open with seafood, brodet, soparnik. Producers Toreta · Bire · Krajančić
  <card drink:🍷 Tribidrag (Crljenak Kaštelanski / Zinfandel)> Kaštela · ancestral grape — the original Zinfandel rootstock See the funfact. In the 1990s only 9 known vines remained in Dalmatia. Today, several producers are rebuilding the vineyards — Putalj (Kaštel Sućurac, 15 km from Split), Mihovil, Brkić. Generally more fluid in style than Plavac Mali, closer to Italian Primitivo than to California Zinfandel. Worth tasting for the historical curiosity as much as for the wine.
  <card drink:🍷 Vugava — indigenous Vis grape variety> Vis island · small production — rare grape, perfectly suited to the island climate Indigenous white grape almost exclusive to Vis island (the most remote in Dalmatia). Gives structured, saline, slightly fat whites that age well. Very limited production. Reference: Lipanović, Roki's, PZ Vis (cooperative). A bottle to take home for lovers of niche whites.
  <card drink:🍇 Prošek — vin de paille dalmate> Straw-dried grapes · sweet · residual sugar — served with rožata or as apéritif Dalmatian sweet wine made from grapes dried on racks (passito method). Cousin of the Jura's Vin de Paille, Friuli's Picolit, Venice's Recioto. Varieties: Plavac Mali for red, Maraština or Bogdanuša for white. High residual sugar, 14° alcohol. Served as apéritif, at meal's end, or used in cooking for pašticada. The legal dispute with Italy over the «Prosek/Prosecco» name is ongoing — don't confuse them.
  <card drink:🌲 Rakija — eau-de-vie de fruit ou d'herbes> Šljivovica, travarica, kruškovac · 40° — apéritif and digestif — whatever the house has on hand Balkan family of eaux-de-vie. Šljivovica — plum, the standard; travarica — herb maceration (sage, rosemary, thyme, wild fennel, up to 30 plants), Dalmatian specialty; kruškovac — pear; orahovac — green walnuts; medenica — honey. Served everywhere, at all hours, on every occasion. The Dalmatian peasant version sold by the maker holds its own. Decent industrial brand: Maraska (since 1899, Zadar). Buy direct from the producer or at the market.
  <card drink:Paradox Wine & Cheese Bar> Centre · €6–12 a glass — the Croatian-wine reference in Split · sommelier who explains plainly See the entry . Wide Croatian wine list, from Pelješac (Plavac Mali) cellars to Plešivica (continental, sparkling, Pinot Gris). Cheese boards (Paški sir, Skuta, Tilan). Sommelier who explains plainly. Open late. The after-dinner stop.
  <card drink:☕ D16 Coffee — troisième vague> Palace · €2–5 espresso — one of the only serious specialty coffees on the Dalmatian coast See the entry . A third-wave pioneer in Split, serious roastery. Small shop in a palace alley. V60, espresso, flat white. Croatian or Ethiopian beans. The serious morning caffeine stop.

[culture]
Culture Fixed stops — inside and around Diocletian's Palace 7 places Diocletian's Palace is the largest Roman building still inhabited in the world. Built between 295 and 305 AD as the retirement palace-fortress of emperor Diocletian (ruled 305–313), 38,000 m², a 215 × 180 m rectangle. In the 7th century, fleeing the Avar-Slav invasions, the inhabitants of nearby Salona took refuge inside the walls — and never left. 3,000 people still live there today, in apartments interlocked between 4th-century columns. UNESCO 1979.
  <card culture:Palais de Dioclétien> UNESCO 1979 · free — 215 × 180 m rectangular plan, 4th c. Free (access) · €4–6 for cathedral, substructures, baptistery See the funfact. Four original gates: Golden Gate (north), Silver Gate (east), Bronze Gate (south, sea-facing), Iron Gate (west). The peristyle (colonnaded court) at the heart. The substructures are almost entirely preserved — Game of Thrones episodes 8–10 were filmed here. Cathedral (Diocletian's former octagonal mausoleum, ironically reconverted to a Christian church in the 7th century), Temple of Jupiter (now the baptistery). What to see Peristyle · substructures · cathedral · baptistery

[secret-gems]
SECRET GEMS — Where locals sit when they want to be alone Bačvice at 6am, Marjan in winter, the peristyle on a November evening 5 spots Split is a living Roman city: Diocletian's palace (305 AD) inhabited for 17 centuries, that empties for two hours a day — at dawn and at midnight. That's when it lets itself be approached.
  <card secret-gems:🌊 Bačvice · pointe est de galets · 06:00> Bačvice beach · past the last cabin — fishermen mending their nets · no tourists before 09:00 Bačvice beach is Split's icon — fine sand, shallow water, the home ground of picigin (local game: a ball that must never touch the water). At 6am, walk past the cabins east to the pebble point: one or two fishermen mending nets in silence. The sun rises over Mosor (the massif behind), Hvar takes on a rose-ash color. Not a tourist before 9am.
  <card secret-gems:🌉 Le péristyle · minuit en novembre> Heart of Diocletian's Palace · after 23:30 — cold Egyptian marbles · a 1500 BC sphinx Diocletian's peristyle is the palace's central court (305 AD). In tourist daytime, it's a scrum. But at midnight in November, after the last waiters leave, you can sit on the Vestibule steps, beneath the Egyptian black-granite sphinx (15th c. BC, reused by Diocletian). The marble keeps the sea's cold. The acoustic resonance is made for voice: this is where the klape sing a cappella in summer.
  <card secret-gems:🌲 Marjan · sentier sud · sortie Sustipan> Marjan · southwest path 30 min — cypresses, Aleppo pines, Brač at sunrise Marjan hill is Split's lung (the "Central Park of Split" locals say, but older). The south path, starting from Sustipan park (former Venetian cemetery, dismantled), runs above the sea. At 7am, the first locals walk their dogs. Stone benches every 200 m. At the end, a clean view of Brač cut against the dawn. When 06:30–08:00
  <card secret-gems:🍷 Matejuška · evening · vin au gobelet> Small fishermen's port · west of the Riva · evening — locals gather here with a bottle from the Pazar Matejuška is Split's small fishermen's harbor — at the western end of the Riva, where the boats line up on stone. No bar, no service: you sit on the stone blocks, bring your bottle from the Pazar (direct-from-producer wine, €5/L), a piece of pršut, a Kaštela tomato. It's Split's 7pm social ritual, off season. In summer, it's younger and denser.
  <card secret-gems:🏛 Toits du palais depuis le campanile de Saint-Domnius> Cathedral · Romanesque-Gothic bell tower · morning — the Roman grid still readable · Mosor in the distance The campanile of St Domnius (13th–16th c., rebuilt in the 19th) dominates the palace. Climb at opening (8am): you see the Roman grid of the palace, still perfectly legible 1,700 years later, and behind it the Mosor massif (1,339 m), often snow-capped in winter. €7. Steep stairs — not for claustrophobes, even less for vertigo sufferers. Empty until 9:30am. When 08:00–09:00

STREET FOOD — The quick local food that stands up Burek at 6am, charcoal ćevapi, fritule from the window, Bilan sandwich 4 entries Dalmatia has a real stand-up bite culture: burek at 6am after the clubs, charcoal-grilled ćevapi (Bosnian sausage-patties), fritule in winter (round doughnuts), and Bilan's sailor's sandwich at noon.
  <card street-food:🤬 Burek du morning · Pekarna Bobis · Marmontova> Pekarna Bobis · opens 6am — burek meso, sirnica cheese, krumpiruša potato · fresh phyllo dough ~2 € Pekarna Bobis is Split's historic bakery chain — bought 1980, several locations including a flagship on Marmontova. [avoid]
Avoid — local-knowledge traps Three things a seasoned traveller still gets wrong in Split 3 traps
  <card avoid:⚠ Unmarked «house wine» on the peristyle> Peristyle terraces — poured-by-the-pitcher pours that are bulk Macedonian, not Croatian Several palace bars pour an «our house wine» from anonymous pitchers — almost always bulk industrial Macedonian or low-grade Vranac, marked up to Plavac Mali prices. Ask for a labelled bottle (Plavac Mali, Pošip), or walk to Paradox or Zinfandel for the real card.
  <card avoid:⚠ Confusing Prošek with Prosecco> Tourist menus and gift shops — two unrelated wines, one name-collision Croatian Prošek is a sweet straw-dried wine (cousin of Vin de Paille and Picolit) — nothing to do with Italian Prosecco , the dry sparkling Glera. Some shops and waiters exploit the confusion; some sell «Prošek» that is in fact thinned-down sweet plonk. Ask explicitly: «sweet, dessert wine» or «sparkling, dry». The legal dispute between Italy and Croatia over the protected name has been ongoing since 2021.
  <card avoid:⚠ Pašticada served at peak summer in tourist tavernas> Riva and palace cards in July–August — a winter Sunday dish, microwaved to order Pašticada is a 24-hour vinegar marinade and a four-hour braise — a winter Sunday cathedral dish. In peak summer, almost no honest kitchen actually makes it from cold; what arrives at €25 in the palace cards is reheated stock from a sous-vide bag. Order it only at houses you can name: Apetit , Fife , an inland konoba — or wait until November.
