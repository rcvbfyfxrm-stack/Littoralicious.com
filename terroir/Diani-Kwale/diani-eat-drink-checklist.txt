DIANI — EAT & DRINK CHECKLIST (littoralicious.com/terroir/Diani-Kwale)

[ ] 1. Madafu (say: mah-DAH-foo)
     The green drinking coconut, machete-opened on the sand; hand it back after and ask for the kuna — the shell-slice spoon.
     Best: Any vendor with a panga; Kongo Beach by the mosque is the classic

[ ] 2. Mnazi (say: m-NAH-zee)
     Coconut palm wine, tapped morning and evening from the cut flower spathe — sweet and fizzing fresh, sour by dusk; the ferment never stops.
     Best: Guided only — the Kaaribu tour's mangwe stop, or the Kaya Kinondo village tasting

[ ] 3. Samaki wa kupaka (say: sah-MAH-kee wah koo-PAH-kah)
     Whole reef fish char-grilled, then smeared in spiced coconut cream and finished over the coals.
     Best: Ukunda's Swahili kitchens; Coast Dishes when open

[ ] 4. Pweza wa nazi (say: PWEH-zah wah NAH-zee)
     Octopus simmered in coconut milk — the reef's toughest catch made silk.
     Best: Karafuu, Ukunda town; or off the boats at Mwaepe

[ ] 5. Mahamri na mbaazi (say: mah-HAHM-ree nah m-BAH-zee)
     Cardamom-coconut doughnut triangles fried in coconut oil, dipped in pigeon peas stewed in coconut milk.
     Best: Ukunda's dawn mama stalls; Coast Dishes for the seated version

[ ] 6. Pilau ya pwani (say: pee-LAU yah PWAH-nee)
     Rice browned in stock with cumin, cardamom and cloves — the coast's celebration pot.
     Best: Swahili Pot or Moiz in Ukunda; Fridays are the best bet

[ ] 7. Coast biryani (say: bir-YAH-nee)
     The layered, wetter Swahili-coast cousin — rice with the mchuzi poured over.
     Best: Moiz Swahili Dishes, Ukunda — early, while it is fresh

[ ] 8. Viazi karai (say: vee-AH-zee kah-RYE)
     Boiled potatoes in turmeric gram-flour batter, deep-fried in the karai, doused in chilli-tamarind ukwaju.
     Best: Ukunda junction vendors from late afternoon

[ ] 9. Mishkaki (say: mish-KAH-kee)
     Charcoal skewers of beef or goat, garlic-ginger-lime marinade — an evening food by convention.
     Best: Ukunda junction after sunset

[ ] 10. Whole grilled reef fish (say: sah-MAH-kee)
     Changu or snapper off the morning ngalawa, judged by eye and gill, grilled over charcoal at the landing.
     Best: Colobus Shade or the fishermen's own table at Mwaepe landing

[ ] 11. The Wasini crab lunch (say: KAH-ah — crab)
     Grilled crab bought daily off the dugouts, eaten under a palm roof over the channel — the dhow day's table since 1978.
     Best: Charlie Claw's, Wasini Island — as part of the full dhow day

[ ] 12. Kuku choma (say: KOO-koo CHO-mah)
     Charcoal-grilled chicken with kachumbari — the town benchmark order.
     Best: Karafuu, Ukunda town, evening off the grill

[ ] 13. Mchicha wa nazi (say: m-CHEE-chah wah NAH-zee)
     Greens simmered in coconut milk — the side that explains the whole kitchen.
     Best: Karafuu or any Swahili canteen in Ukunda

[ ] 14. Mango, Ngowe (say: n-GO-weh)
     The coast's slender, fibreless classic mango — in season roughly November to January.
     Best: Roadside fruit tables and the Mvindeni market, Dec–Jan

[ ] 15. Kilifi oysters (say: oysters)
     Kenyan-coast rock oysters, farmed up the coast at Kilifi.
     Best: Ali Barbour's Cave; Sails when the coast supplies

[ ] 16. Sugarcane juice (say: muah — sugarcane)
     Pressed to order at Ukunda town stands — ask for lime and ginger.
     Best: Ukunda town stands, mornings

[ ] 17. Smoked Negroni (say: neh-GRO-nee)
     The house twist at the coast's only 50 Best Discovery bar.
     Best: Funky Monkey, Wednesdays for salsa night

[ ] 18. Kentaste coconut cream (say: ken-TAST-eh)
     The take-home: coconut cream pressed minutes from the beach, 30,000 nuts a day from 4,000 Kwale smallholders.
     Best: Carrefour Diani or Chandarana, by the case
